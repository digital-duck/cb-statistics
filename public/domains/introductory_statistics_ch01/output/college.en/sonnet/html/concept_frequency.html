Frequency

Definition. Let a periodic phenomenon complete $n$ full cycles in a time interval of duration $T$ seconds. The frequency $f$ of that phenomenon is defined as

$$f = \frac{n}{T},$$

measured in hertz (Hz), where $1\,\text{Hz} = 1\,\text{cycle per second}$. Equivalently, if $T_0$ denotes the period—the duration of exactly one cycle—then

$$f = \frac{1}{T_0}.$$

In the context of a sinusoidal signal $x(t) = A\sin(2\pi f t + \phi)$, the quantity $\omega = 2\pi f$ is called the angular frequency, expressed in radians per second. The three quantities $f$, $T_0$, and $\omega$ carry identical information and are related by $\omega T_0 = 2\pi$.

Worked Example. A sound wave completes $440$ cycles each second (the musical note A4). Its period is

$$T_0 = \frac{1}{440\,\text{Hz}} \approx 2.27 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s},$$

and its angular frequency is

$$\omega = 2\pi \times 440 \approx 2764.6\,\text{rad s}^{-1}.$$

A microphone records the pressure variation $p(t) = 0.02\sin(2\pi \cdot 440\, t)$ Pa. The amplitude is $0.02$ Pa, the frequency is $440$ Hz, and the phase offset $\phi = 0$.

Key Theorem (Nyquist–Shannon Sampling Theorem). If a continuous signal contains no frequency components above a maximum frequency $f_{\max}$, then the signal is completely determined by—and can be perfectly reconstructed from—discrete samples taken at any rate $f_s$ satisfying

$$f_s > 2f_{\max}.$$

The threshold $f_N = f_{\max}$ is called the Nyquist frequency. Sampling below this rate introduces aliasing, wherein high-frequency components masquerade as spurious lower frequencies, corrupting the signal irreversibly.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, sin, pi, plot, Rational

t = symbols('t', real=True)
f = 440          # Hz
signal = sin(2 * pi * f * t)

# Inspect period and angular frequency symbolically
T0 = Rational(1, f)
omega = 2 * pi * f
print(f"Period T0 = {T0} s,  angular frequency ω = {omega} rad/s")

plot(signal, (t, 0, 3/f), xlabel='t (s)', ylabel='p(t)',
     title='440 Hz sinusoid (3 cycles)')

Running this cell confirms $T_0 = 1/440$ s and renders three complete oscillations, making the period–frequency relationship directly observable.
