Z Score Confidence

Definition. Recall that when $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ are independent observations from a population with mean $\mu$ and known standard deviation $\sigma$, the standardized sample mean $Z = (\bar{X} - \mu)/(\sigma/\sqrt{n})$ follows a standard normal distribution, exactly for normal populations and approximately for large $n$ by the Central Limit Theorem. Building on this, a z-score confidence interval for $\mu$ at confidence level $1 - \alpha$ is the interval

$$\bar{X} \;\pm\; z_{\alpha/2}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}},$$

where $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the upper $\alpha/2$ critical value of the standard normal, satisfying $P(Z \leq z_{\alpha/2}) = 1 - \alpha/2$. This is the single new object introduced here: an interval, constructed from observed data, designed so that the true mean is captured with a prescribed probability.

Worked Example. A quality-control engineer measures the fill volume (in mL) of $n = 64$ bottles. The sample mean is $\bar{X} = 502.3$ mL and the known population standard deviation is $\sigma = 4.0$ mL. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the true mean fill $\mu$.

For $1 - \alpha = 0.95$, we have $\alpha/2 = 0.025$, giving $z_{0.025} = 1.96$. The margin of error is

$$E = 1.96 \cdot \frac{4.0}{\sqrt{64}} = 1.96 \cdot 0.5 = 0.98 \text{ mL}.$$

The 95% confidence interval is therefore $[501.32,\; 503.28]$ mL. We interpret this as: if this procedure were repeated on many independent samples of size 64, approximately 95% of the resulting intervals would contain the true mean $\mu$.

Key Theorem (Coverage Guarantee). Under the assumptions above, the random interval $\bigl[\bar{X} - z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma/\!\sqrt{n},\; \bar{X} + z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma/\!\sqrt{n}\bigr]$ satisfies

$$P\!\left(\mu \in \left[\bar{X} - z_{\alpha/2}\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}},\;\bar{X} + z_{\alpha/2}\tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\right) = 1 - \alpha.$$

The probability is over the randomness in $\bar{X}$; once data are observed, $\mu$ is fixed and the interval either contains it or does not. Confidence is a property of the procedure, not of a single realized interval.

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp

x_bar, sigma, n, z = 502.3, 4.0, 64, 1.96
se = sigma / sp.sqrt(n)
lower = sp.Rational(x_bar) - z * se
upper = sp.Rational(x_bar) + z * se
print(f"95% CI: [{float(lower):.4f}, {float(upper):.4f}] mL")
