Chi Square Distribution

Definition. Let $Z_1, Z_2, \ldots, Z_k$ be independent standard normal random variables, each satisfying $Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$. The random variable

$$X = \sum_{i=1}^{k} Z_i^2$$

follows the chi-square distribution with $k$ degrees of freedom, written $X \sim \chi^2(k)$. Its probability density function is

$$f(x;\,k) = \frac{x^{k/2 - 1}\,e^{-x/2}}{2^{k/2}\,\Gamma(k/2)}, \qquad x > 0,$$

where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ denotes the gamma function. The distribution has mean $\mathbb{E}[X] = k$ and variance $\operatorname{Var}(X) = 2k$. As $k$ increases, the distribution becomes increasingly symmetric and approaches a normal distribution by the central limit theorem.

Worked Example. Suppose a quality-control engineer measures the fill volume of beverage cans. Under the null hypothesis, fill volume is normally distributed with known mean $\mu_0 = 355\,\text{mL}$ and known standard deviation $\sigma = 2\,\text{mL}$. A sample of $k = 5$ cans yields standardised residuals $z_1 = 1.2,\; z_2 = -0.8,\; z_3 = 0.5,\; z_4 = -1.5,\; z_5 = 0.9$. The test statistic is

$$\chi^2 = (1.2)^2 + (-0.8)^2 + (0.5)^2 + (-1.5)^2 + (0.9)^2 = 1.44 + 0.64 + 0.25 + 2.25 + 0.81 = 5.39.$$

Comparing to the critical value $\chi^2_{0.05,\,5} \approx 11.07$, the engineer fails to reject the null hypothesis at the 5% significance level.

Key Theorem (Additivity). If $X_1 \sim \chi^2(k_1)$ and $X_2 \sim \chi^2(k_2)$ are independent, then

$$X_1 + X_2 \sim \chi^2(k_1 + k_2).$$

Proof sketch. The moment-generating function of $\chi^2(k)$ is $M(t) = (1 - 2t)^{-k/2}$ for $t < \tfrac{1}{2}$. Independence gives $M_{X_1+X_2}(t) = M_{X_1}(t)\cdot M_{X_2}(t) = (1-2t)^{-(k_1+k_2)/2}$, which identifies the sum as $\chi^2(k_1+k_2)$. $\square$

from sympy import symbols, exp, sqrt, pi, gamma, integrate, simplify, Rational

x, k = symbols('x k', positive=True)

# PDF for k=3 degrees of freedom
k_val = 3
pdf = x**(k_val/2 - 1) * exp(-x/2) / (2**(k_val/2) * gamma(Rational(k_val, 2)))

# Verify normalisation
norm = integrate(pdf, (x, 0, float('inf')))
print("Integral of PDF (k=3):", simplify(norm))   # expects 1

# Compute mean analytically
mean = integrate(x * pdf, (x, 0, float('inf')))
print("Mean (k=3):", simplify(mean))               # expects 3
