Concept Book: Real World Two Group Comparisons

Payoff

Definition. A real-world two-group comparison is a statistical procedure that quantifies evidence for a difference in a population parameter between two distinct groups, based on independent or paired samples drawn under controlled or observational conditions. Formally, given samples $X_1, \ldots, X_m \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} F_1$ and $Y_1, \ldots, Y_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} F_2$, the procedure tests the null hypothesis $H_0: \theta(F_1) = \theta(F_2)$ against a directional or omnibus alternative, where $\theta$ is a functional of interest—commonly the mean $\mu$, median, or proportion $p$.

This concept is the natural endpoint of the textbook's arc because every preceding tool—probability distributions, sampling theory, point estimation, confidence intervals, and the central limit theorem—converges here into a single, decision-bearing conclusion. Two-group comparison is not merely a technique; it is the grammar through which empirical science asks whether an intervention, a treatment, or a structural difference matters.

Worked Example. A clinical trial enrolls $m = 40$ patients receiving a new analgesic and $n = 40$ receiving placebo. Let $\bar{X} = 3.2$ and $\bar{Y} = 4.7$ (pain scores, lower is better), with sample standard deviations $s_X = 1.1$ and $s_Y = 1.3$. The two-sample $t$-statistic is

$$t = \frac{\bar{X} - \bar{Y}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{s_X^2}{m} + \dfrac{s_Y^2}{n}}} = \frac{3.2 - 4.7}{\sqrt{\dfrac{1.21}{40} + \dfrac{1.69}{40}}} \approx \frac{-1.5}{0.265} \approx -5.66.$$

With degrees of freedom approximated by the Welch–Satterthwaite formula ($\nu \approx 76$), this $t$-value yields $p < 0.001$, providing strong evidence that the analgesic reduces pain relative to placebo.

Key Theorem (Welch's $t$-Test Validity). Under $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$, if $F_1$ and $F_2$ have finite variances $\sigma_1^2$ and $\sigma_2^2$ (not necessarily equal), then the statistic $t$ above converges in distribution to $t_\nu$ as $m, n \to \infty$, where $\nu$ is the Welch–Satterthwaite approximation. At finite sample sizes, the Type I error rate remains well-controlled whenever both populations are approximately normal or both sample sizes exceed 30 by the central limit theorem.

Lab Cell.

from scipy import stats
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
analgesic = rng.normal(loc=3.2, scale=1.1, size=40)
placebo   = rng.normal(loc=4.7, scale=1.3, size=40)

result = stats.ttest_ind(analgesic, placebo, equal_var=False)
print(f"t = {result.statistic:.3f},  p = {result.pvalue:.4f}")

Run this cell and observe that the $p$-value falls below the conventional threshold $\alpha = 0.05$. Modify loc to shrink the true difference toward zero and watch the $p$-value rise, developing your intuition for statistical power.
