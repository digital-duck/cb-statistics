Variance

Variance is a fundamental measure of dispersion that quantifies how spread out a set of data points are relative to their mean. While standard deviation simply reports this spread in the original units of measurement ($\sigma$), variance reports it in squared units ($\sigma^2$). Conceptually, we calculate variance by determining the average of the squared differences between each data point and the mean ($\mu$ or $\bar{x}$). Since squaring the deviations ensures that positive and negative distances from the mean do not cancel each other out, variance provides a single, powerful metric for measuring overall variability.

The calculation differs slightly depending on whether you are analyzing an entire population (using $\sigma^2$) or merely using a sample drawn from a larger population (using $s^2$). For a population of size $N$, the variance is defined as:

$$ \sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} (x_i - \mu)^2}{N} $$

For a sample of size $n$, we use Bessel’s correction to provide an unbiased estimate, adjusting the denominator:

$$ s^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n-1} $$
