Concept Book: Dice Experiment

Introductory Statistics Ch04 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A dice experiment is the canonical finite probability space: a sample space $\Omega = \{1, 2, \dots, 6\}^n$ for $n$ independent rolls of a fair die, equipped with the uniform measure $P(\{\omega\}) = 6^{-n}$ for every elementary outcome $\omega \in \Omega$. Despite its apparent simplicity, this construction is the natural endpoint of the theory developed throughout this text, because it simultaneously instantiates every foundational object studied—sample spaces, $\sigma$-algebras, random variables, expectation, variance, and the limiting theorems—in a setting small enough to verify by hand and large enough to surprise.

Worked example. Let $X_i$ denote the outcome of the $i$-th roll, $i = 1, \dots, n$. Define the sample mean $\bar{X}_n = n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. For a single fair die, $\mu = E[X_i] = 7/2$ and $\sigma^2 = \mathrm{Var}(X_i) = 35/12$. By direct enumeration for $n = 2$ one confirms $P(\bar{X}_2 = 3.5) = 6/36 = 1/6$, matching the symmetry of the distribution about its mean.

Key theorem (Weak Law of Large Numbers applied). For the dice experiment with $n$ rolls,

$$P\!\left(\left|\bar{X}_n - \frac{7}{2}\right| \geq \varepsilon\right) \;\leq\; \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2} = \frac{35}{12\,n\varepsilon^2}, \qquad \varepsilon > 0.$$

This Chebyshev bound, proved in Chapter 6, guarantees that $\bar{X}_n \xrightarrow{P} 7/2$ as $n \to \infty$. The Central Limit Theorem sharpens the picture: the standardised mean $Z_n = \sqrt{n}(\bar{X}_n - 7/2)/\sqrt{35/12}$ converges in distribution to $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, a fact visible in the bell-shaped histograms generated in the lab below.

Lab cell (SymPy / Python).

from sympy.stats import Die, E, variance, sample
from sympy import Rational, sqrt, simplify

X = Die('X', 6)
mu  = E(X)          # 7/2
sig2 = variance(X)  # 35/12
n = 1000
rolls = [int(sample(X)) for _ in range(n)]
x_bar = Rational(sum(rolls), n)
print(f"Theoretical mean: {mu},  Sample mean: {float(x_bar):.4f}")
print(f"Chebyshev bound (ε=0.1): {float(sig2 / (n * Rational(1,100))):.5f}")

Running this cell turns theorem into observation: the sample mean clusters tightly around $7/2$, and the Chebyshev bound quantifies exactly how tightly.

The dice experiment is the natural capstone because it closes every conceptual loop opened in Chapter 1.
