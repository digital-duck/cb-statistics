Law Of Large Numbers

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables, each with finite mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_i]$ and finite variance $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(X_i)$. Define the sample mean

$$\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i.$$

The Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) states that for every $\varepsilon > 0$,

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} P\!\left(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| > \varepsilon\right) = 0.$$

That is, $\bar{X}_n$ converges in probability to $\mu$. The Strong Law sharpens this to almost-sure convergence: $P\!\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{X}_n = \mu\right) = 1$.

Intuitively, random fluctuations in individual observations cancel as sample size grows, causing the empirical average to stabilise at the theoretical mean.

Worked Example. Roll a fair six-sided die repeatedly. Each roll $X_i$ has mean $\mu = \tfrac{1+2+3+4+5+6}{6} = 3.5$. After $n = 10$ rolls you might observe $\bar{X}_{10} = 3.8$; after $n = 1000$ rolls the sample mean will almost certainly lie within $0.1$ of $3.5$. The WLLN guarantees that however small you choose $\varepsilon > 0$, the probability of deviating by more than $\varepsilon$ from $3.5$ vanishes as $n \to \infty$.

Key Theorem (Chebyshev proof of WLLN). Because $\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_n] = \mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \sigma^2/n$, Chebyshev's inequality gives

$$P\!\left(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| > \varepsilon\right) \leq \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_n)}{\varepsilon^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}.$$

As $n \to \infty$ the right-hand side tends to $0$ for any fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, completing the proof of the WLLN under the finite-variance assumption. $\blacksquare$

Lab Cell (SymPy / NumPy).

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
ns  = np.arange(1, 5001)
rolls = rng.integers(1, 7, size=5000)          # fair die: 1–6
running_mean = np.cumsum(rolls) / ns

plt.plot(ns, running_mean, label=r"$\bar{X}_n$")
plt.axhline(3.5, color="red", linestyle="--", label=r"$\mu = 3.5$")
plt.xlabel("$n$")
plt.ylabel("Sample mean")
plt.title("Convergence of sample mean (LLN)")
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Run this cell and observe how the blue curve hugs the red line $\mu = 3.5$ ever more tightly as $n$ increases, providing a visual confirmation of the law of large numbers.
