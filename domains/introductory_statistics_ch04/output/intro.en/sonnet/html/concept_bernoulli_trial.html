Bernoulli Trial

Definition. A Bernoulli trial is a single random experiment that produces exactly two mutually exclusive outcomes, conventionally labeled success (S) and failure (F). The probability of success is a fixed constant $p \in [0,1]$, and the probability of failure is $q = 1 - p$. Formally, a Bernoulli trial is a discrete random variable $X$ with probability mass function

$$P(X = k) = p^k (1-p)^{1-k}, \quad k \in \{0, 1\},$$

where $X = 1$ encodes success and $X = 0$ encodes failure. The distribution is denoted $X \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$.

Worked Example. Consider flipping a fair coin. Define success as "heads." Then $p = 0.5$ and $q = 0.5$. Each individual flip is one Bernoulli trial. The probability of observing heads on a single flip is

$$P(X = 1) = (0.5)^1 (0.5)^0 = 0.5,$$

and the probability of tails is $P(X = 0) = 0.5$. Now suppose the coin is biased, with $p = 0.3$. The probability of heads drops to $0.3$, while failure (tails) carries probability $0.7$. The pmf formula handles both cases uniformly without requiring separate notation.

Key Theorem — Mean and Variance. For $X \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$,

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = p \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathrm{Var}(X) = p(1-p).$$

Proof sketch. By direct computation, $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0 \cdot (1-p) + 1 \cdot p = p$. For the variance, $\mathbb{E}[X^2] = 0^2(1-p) + 1^2 p = p$, so $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2 = p - p^2 = p(1-p)$. Note that variance is maximised at $p = 0.5$, reflecting maximum uncertainty when both outcomes are equally likely.

The Bernoulli trial is the foundational building block of probability theory. Sequences of independent, identically distributed Bernoulli trials generate the Binomial distribution, and in the limit of many rare trials they converge to the Poisson distribution — making this deceptively simple model the seed of an entire family of distributions encountered throughout statistics and machine learning.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, binomial, Rational, simplify
from sympy.stats import Bernoulli, E, variance

p = Rational(3, 10)          # biased coin: P(heads) = 0.3
X = Bernoulli('X', p)

print("E[X]   =", E(X))      # Expected value
print("Var(X) =", variance(X))  # Variance p(1-p)

Running this cell confirms $\mathbb{E}[X] = 3/10$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X) = 21/100$, matching the analytical formulas exactly.
