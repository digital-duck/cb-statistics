Test Single Mean Unknown Sd

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be an independent and identically distributed random sample from a normal population with unknown mean $\mu$ and unknown standard deviation $\sigma$. The one-sample t-test provides a procedure for testing a hypothesis about $\mu$ when $\sigma$ must be estimated from the data. The test statistic is

$$t = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{S / \sqrt{n}},$$

where $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ is the sample mean, $S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2}$ is the sample standard deviation, and $\mu_0$ is the hypothesized population mean. Under the null hypothesis $H_0: \mu = \mu_0$, the statistic $t$ follows a Student's $t$-distribution with $\nu = n - 1$ degrees of freedom.

Worked Example. A nutritionist claims that a new energy bar contains exactly 250 kcal per serving. A quality-control analyst measures $n = 16$ bars and records $\bar{X} = 261$ kcal and $S = 20$ kcal. Test $H_0: \mu = 250$ against $H_1: \mu \neq 250$ at significance level $\alpha = 0.05$.

The observed test statistic is

$$t_{\text{obs}} = \frac{261 - 250}{20 / \sqrt{16}} = \frac{11}{5} = 2.20.$$

With $\nu = 15$ degrees of freedom, the two-tailed critical value is $t_{0.025, 15} \approx 2.131$. Since $|t_{\text{obs}}| = 2.20 > 2.131$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude that the mean caloric content differs significantly from 250 kcal.

Key Theorem. If $X_1, \ldots, X_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, then $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ are independent, and

$$\frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S/\sqrt{n}} \sim t_{n-1}.$$

This result follows from the independence of $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ (Cochran's theorem) and the representation of a $t$-distributed variable as a standard normal divided by the square root of an independent chi-squared variable scaled by its degrees of freedom.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from scipy import stats
import numpy as np

data = [261, 248, 255, 270, 260, 252, 263, 258,
        265, 245, 257, 272, 250, 269, 253, 261]

t_stat, p_value = stats.ttest_1samp(data, popmean=250)
print(f"t = {t_stat:.4f},  p = {p_value:.4f}")

# Confidence interval
ci = stats.t.interval(0.95, df=len(data)-1,
                      loc=np.mean(data),
                      scale=stats.sem(data))
print(f"95% CI: ({ci[0]:.2f}, {ci[1]:.2f})")
