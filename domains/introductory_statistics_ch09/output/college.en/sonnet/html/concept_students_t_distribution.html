Students T Distribution

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be an independent and identically distributed random sample from a normal population $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with unknown variance. Define the sample mean $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and the sample variance $S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2$. The Student's t-statistic is

$$T = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S / \sqrt{n}}.$$

This statistic follows a Student's t-distribution with $\nu = n - 1$ degrees of freedom, written $T \sim t(\nu)$. Its probability density function is

$$f(t;\,\nu) = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\;\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)} \left(1 + \frac{t^2}{\nu}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}, \quad t \in \mathbb{R}.$$

As $\nu \to \infty$ the distribution converges to $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$; for small $\nu$ the tails are substantially heavier, reflecting the additional uncertainty introduced by estimating $\sigma$.

Worked Example. A metallurgist measures the tensile strength (MPa) of five steel samples: $\{412, 418, 405, 421, 409\}$. Compute $\bar{X} = 413$ and $S \approx 6.20$. To test $H_0: \mu = 410$ at the 5% significance level (two-tailed), form

$$t_{\text{obs}} = \frac{413 - 410}{6.20 / \sqrt{5}} = \frac{3}{2.773} \approx 1.082.$$

With $\nu = 4$ degrees of freedom the critical value is $t_{0.025,4} = 2.776$. Since $|t_{\text{obs}}| = 1.082 < 2.776$, we fail to reject $H_0$; the data do not provide sufficient evidence that the mean tensile strength differs from 410 MPa.

Key Theorem (Cochran, 1934). If $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ are the sample mean and variance of an i.i.d. normal sample of size $n$, then $\bar{X}$ and $S^2$ are independent, and $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2(n-1)$. It follows directly that $T = (\bar{X}-\mu)/(S/\sqrt{n})$ is the ratio of a standard normal to the square root of an independent $\chi^2(n-1)$ variable divided by its degrees of freedom — precisely the construction defining $t(\nu)$.

from sympy import symbols, gamma, sqrt, pi, simplify, oo, limit

t, nu = symbols('t nu', positive=True)

# t-distribution PDF
pdf = (gamma((nu + 1) / 2) / (sqrt(nu * pi) * gamma(nu / 2))) * \
      (1 + t**2 / nu) ** (-(nu + 1) / 2)

# Verify convergence to standard normal density as nu -> oo
normal_pdf = (1 / sqrt(2 * pi)) * sympy.exp(-t**2 / 2)
print(simplify(limit(pdf, nu, oo) - normal_pdf))  # expect 0
