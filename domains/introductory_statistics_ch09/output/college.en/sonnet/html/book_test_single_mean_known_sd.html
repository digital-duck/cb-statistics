Concept Book: Test Single Mean Known Sd

Introductory Statistics Ch09 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the test achieves. Every chapter of this book has built toward a single act of disciplined inference: deciding, on the basis of a finite sample, whether a population mean departs from a claimed value. The procedure test_single_mean_known_sd formalises that act. Given a random sample $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ drawn from a population with unknown mean $\mu$ and known standard deviation $\sigma$, it tests the null hypothesis $H_0\colon \mu = \mu_0$ against an alternative of the experimenter's choosing. The test statistic

$$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$

follows a standard normal distribution exactly under $H_0$ whenever the population is normal, and approximately for large $n$ by the Central Limit Theorem. A $p$-value is then $p = 2\,\Phi(-|z|)$ for a two-sided test, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Rejecting $H_0$ when $p < \alpha$ controls the Type I error rate at level $\alpha$.

Worked example. A bottling plant claims fill volume $\mu_0 = 500\,\text{mL}$ with known process standard deviation $\sigma = 4\,\text{mL}$. A quality inspector measures $n = 25$ bottles and records $\bar{x} = 498.4\,\text{mL}$. The test statistic is

$$z = \frac{498.4 - 500}{4/\sqrt{25}} = \frac{-1.6}{0.8} = -2.00.$$

The two-sided $p$-value is $2\,\Phi(-2.00) \approx 0.0455$. At significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ the inspector rejects $H_0$ and flags the line for recalibration.

Key theorem. Under $H_0$ the statistic $Z$ defined above has distribution $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, and the level-$\alpha$ test that rejects when $|Z| > z_{\alpha/2}$ is the uniformly most powerful unbiased test of $H_0$ against $H_1\colon \mu \neq \mu_0$, a consequence of the Neyman–Pearson lemma applied to the exponential family structure of the normal model.

Lab cell.

from scipy import stats
import sympy as sp

x_bar, mu_0, sigma, n = 498.4, 500, 4, 25
z = (x_bar - mu_0) / (sigma / n**0.5)
p = 2 * stats.norm.sf(abs(z))
print(f"z = {z:.4f},  p = {p:.4f}")

# Symbolic verification
z_sym = sp.Rational(-8, 4)          # exact value −2
p_sym = 2 * (1 - sp.erf(sp.Abs(z_sym) / sp.sqrt(2))) / 2
sp.pprint(sp.simplify(p_sym))
