Test Single Mean Known Sd

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be a random sample drawn from a population with unknown mean $\mu$ and known population standard deviation $\sigma$. The one-sample $z$-test evaluates the null hypothesis

$$H_0: \mu = \mu_0$$

against one of three alternatives: $H_1: \mu \neq \mu_0$ (two-tailed), $H_1: \mu > \mu_0$ (right-tailed), or $H_1: \mu < \mu_0$ (left-tailed). The test statistic is

$$z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}},$$

where $\bar{X}$ is the sample mean. Under $H_0$, the Central Limit Theorem guarantees that $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ for large $n$ (or exactly when the population is normal). The hypothesis is rejected at significance level $\alpha$ when $|z| > z_{\alpha/2}$ (two-tailed), $z > z_\alpha$ (right-tailed), or $z < -z_\alpha$ (left-tailed), where $z_\alpha$ denotes the upper $\alpha$-quantile of the standard normal distribution.

Worked Example. A bottling plant claims its machines fill bottles to a mean volume of $\mu_0 = 500\,\text{mL}$. Historical records confirm $\sigma = 8\,\text{mL}$. A quality inspector draws $n = 64$ bottles and records $\bar{X} = 497.5\,\text{mL}$. Testing $H_0: \mu = 500$ against $H_1: \mu \neq 500$ at $\alpha = 0.05$:

$$z = \frac{497.5 - 500}{8/\sqrt{64}} = \frac{-2.5}{1} = -2.50.$$

The two-tailed critical value is $z_{0.025} = 1.96$. Since $|{-2.50}| = 2.50 > 1.96$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude there is statistically significant evidence that the true mean fill volume differs from $500\,\text{mL}$.

Key Theorem (Uniformly Most Powerful Test). For a one-sided alternative such as $H_1: \mu > \mu_0$, the $z$-test based on the rejection region $\{z > z_\alpha\}$ is the uniformly most powerful (UMP) level-$\alpha$ test among all unbiased tests when the population is normal with known $\sigma$. This follows from the Neyman–Pearson lemma applied to the exponential family structure of the normal likelihood.

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp

mu0, sigma, n_val = 500, 8, 64
x_bar = sp.Rational(4975, 10)          # exact representation of 497.5

z_stat = (x_bar - mu0) / (sigma / sp.sqrt(n_val))
print(z_stat)                          # -5/2

# norm.cdf is the one new primitive introduced here:
# it returns the cumulative probability P(Z <= z) for Z ~ N(0,1)
from scipy.stats import norm
p_value = 2 * norm.cdf(float(z_stat))
print(round(p_value, 5))               # 0.01242
