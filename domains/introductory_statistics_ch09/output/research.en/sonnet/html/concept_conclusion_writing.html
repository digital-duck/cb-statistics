Conclusion Writing

Definition. A conclusion in a mathematical or scientific argument is the terminal statement $C$ that is logically entailed by a set of premises $\{P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_n\}$ and a chain of inference steps. Formally, we write $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_n \vdash C$, read "the premises derive $C$," to assert that every model satisfying all $P_i$ also satisfies $C$. A well-formed conclusion satisfies three criteria: completeness (it answers the original question in full), minimality (it asserts no more than the premises support), and traceability (each claim maps back to a numbered step in the derivation).

In expository writing, conclusion writing is the craft of translating this logical structure into coherent prose, ensuring that the reader can reconstruct the derivation from the written text alone.

Worked Example. Suppose we wish to conclude that the sequence $a_n = 1/n$ converges to zero. The derivation proceeds: (1) Let $\varepsilon > 0$. (2) Choose $N = \lceil 1/\varepsilon \rceil$. (3) For all $n > N$, we have $|a_n - 0| = 1/n < 1/N \leq \varepsilon$. A minimal, traceable conclusion reads: "Therefore, for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $N$ such that $n > N$ implies $|a_n| < \varepsilon$; hence $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$." Note that the conclusion restates the definition of the limit, names the quantity constructed ($N$), and does not introduce new claims.

Key Theorem (Soundness of Modus Ponens). If $P \Rightarrow Q$ and $P$ are both true, then $Q$ is true. This elementary rule underlies every conclusion: the premises collectively form $P$, the logical chain encodes $P \Rightarrow Q$, and the conclusion asserts $Q$. A conclusion that outstrips its premises violates soundness; a conclusion that under-reports its premises wastes the derivation.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, limit, oo, Abs, simplify

n = symbols('n', positive=True, integer=True)
a_n = 1 / n

lim_val = limit(a_n, n, oo)
print("Limit:", lim_val)           # 0

# Verify the epsilon-N bound symbolically
epsilon = symbols('epsilon', positive=True)
N_choice = 1 / epsilon
residual = simplify(Abs(a_n) - epsilon)
print("Residual at n = N + 1:", residual.subs(n, N_choice + 1))

Running this cell confirms $\lim_{n\to\infty} 1/n = 0$ and shows that the symbolic residual is negative for $n > 1/\varepsilon$, validating the written conclusion against a computer algebra system.
