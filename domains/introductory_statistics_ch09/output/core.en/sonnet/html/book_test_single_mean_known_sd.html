Concept Book: Test Single Mean Known Sd

Introductory Statistics Ch09 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the Test Achieves

The one-sample $z$-test for a known population standard deviation is the cleanest possible instance of statistical inference: a single population mean $\mu$ is unknown, every other parameter is specified, and the practitioner must decide—on the basis of $n$ observations—whether the data are consistent with a hypothesised value $\mu_0$.

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ known. The test statistic for $H_0 : \mu = \mu_0$ is

$$Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}},$$

where $\bar{X} = n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ is the sample mean. Under $H_0$, the Central Limit Theorem guarantees $Z \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ for large $n$ even when the underlying distribution is non-normal, making the procedure broadly applicable. The $p$-value for a two-sided alternative $H_1 : \mu \neq \mu_0$ is $p = 2\Phi(-|Z|)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.

Worked Example

A laboratory thermometer is claimed to read $\mu_0 = 100\,^\circ\text{C}$ at boiling point. Historical calibration gives $\sigma = 0.5\,^\circ\text{C}$. Twenty independent readings yield $\bar{x} = 100.22\,^\circ\text{C}$.

$$Z = \frac{100.22 - 100}{0.5/\sqrt{20}} = \frac{0.22}{0.1118} \approx 1.97.$$

The two-sided $p$-value is $p = 2\Phi(-1.97) \approx 0.049$. At the $\alpha = 0.05$ significance level, the null hypothesis is rejected: the thermometer exhibits a statistically significant systematic bias.

Key Theorem

Neyman–Pearson optimality. Among all level-$\alpha$ tests of $H_0 : \mu = \mu_0$ against $H_1 : \mu = \mu_1 > \mu_0$ with $\sigma$ known, the one-sided $z$-test attains the maximum power $1 - \beta$ at every alternative $\mu_1$. This uniformly most powerful (UMP) property means no competing procedure can do better—the $z$-test is not merely convenient but provably optimal under Gaussian assumptions.

Lab Cell

from sympy.stats import Normal, P, cdf
import sympy as sp

mu0, sigma, n, xbar = 100, sp.Rational(1,2), 20, sp.Rational(11011,50)
Z_val = (xbar - mu0) / (sigma / sp.sqrt(n))
Phi  = cdf(Normal('Z', 0, 1))
p_value = 2 * (1 - Phi(sp.Abs(Z_val)))
sp.pprint(sp.simplify(p_value.rewrite(sp.erfc)))
