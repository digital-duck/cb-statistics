Concept Book: Test Single Proportion

Introductory Statistics Ch09 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the Test Achieves

After building intuition about sampling distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, and the construction of confidence intervals, the one-proportion $z$-test (test_single_proportion) crystallizes everything into a single, decisive inferential act. Given a sample of $n$ independent Bernoulli trials yielding $\hat{p}$ successes, the test asks: is the observed proportion consistent with a hypothesized population proportion $p_0$? The test statistic

$$z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}}$$

follows an approximate standard normal distribution under $H_0$ whenever $np_0 \geq 10$ and $n(1-p_0) \geq 10$. The corresponding $p$-value then quantifies the probability of observing a result at least as extreme as $\hat{p}$, given that $H_0$ is true. Rejection of $H_0$ at significance level $\alpha$ asserts — with controlled type-I error — that the true proportion differs from $p_0$.

Worked Example

A quality-control engineer inspects $n = 400$ manufactured circuit boards and finds $\hat{p} = 0.06$ defective. The specification requires a defect rate no higher than $p_0 = 0.04$. Testing $H_0: p = 0.04$ against $H_1: p > 0.04$:

$$z = \frac{0.06 - 0.04}{\sqrt{0.04 \cdot 0.96 / 400}} = \frac{0.02}{0.0098} \approx 2.04.$$

The one-tailed $p$-value is $P(Z > 2.04) \approx 0.021$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, the engineer rejects $H_0$ and flags the production line for review.

Key Theorem

Normal approximation to the binomial (De Moivre–Laplace). If $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, p)$, then as $n \to \infty$,

$$\frac{X - np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$

This theorem is the theoretical guarantee that $z$ above behaves like a standard normal variate under $H_0$, justifying every critical value and $p$-value computed from the normal table.

Lab Cell

from scipy.stats import binomtest

result = binomtest(k=24, n=400, p=0.04, alternative='greater')
print(f"p-value: {result.pvalue:.4f}")
print(f"95% CI: {result.proportion_ci(confidence_level=0.95)}")
