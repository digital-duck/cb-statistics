Central Limit Theorem

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables, each with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2 > 0$. Define the sample mean $\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) states that the standardized sum

$$Z_n = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$$

converges in distribution to the standard normal $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ as $n \to \infty$. That is, for every $z \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} P(Z_n \leq z) = \Phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{z} e^{-t^2/2}\, dt.$$

Crucially, this holds regardless of the shape of the distribution of each $X_i$, provided the mean and variance are finite.

Worked Example. Suppose a fair die is rolled $n = 60$ times. Each roll $X_i$ has mean $\mu = 3.5$ and variance $\sigma^2 = \frac{35}{12} \approx 2.917$, so $\sigma \approx 1.708$. What is the probability that the sample mean exceeds $3.7$?

Standardize: $z = \frac{3.7 - 3.5}{1.708/\sqrt{60}} = \frac{0.2}{0.2205} \approx 0.907$.

Therefore $P(\bar{X}_{60} > 3.7) \approx 1 - \Phi(0.907) \approx 1 - 0.818 = 0.182$. Despite the discrete, uniform distribution of each roll, the CLT makes normal approximation reliable at $n = 60$.

Theorem (Berry–Esseen bound). The convergence is not merely qualitative. If $E[|X_i|^3] < \infty$ with third absolute moment $\rho = E[|X_i - \mu|^3]$, then

$$\sup_{z \in \mathbb{R}} \left| P(Z_n \leq z) - \Phi(z) \right| \leq \frac{C\,\rho}{\sigma^3 \sqrt{n}},$$

where $C < 0.4748$. This provides an explicit rate of $O(n^{-1/2})$ for the approximation error, justifying practical use for moderate $n$.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
import sympy as sp

# Simulate CLT for Uniform(1,6) — die rolls
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
n, trials = 60, 50_000
rolls = rng.integers(1, 7, size=(trials, n))
sample_means = rolls.mean(axis=1)

mu, sigma2 = 3.5, 35/12
Z = (sample_means - mu) / (sigma2/n)**0.5

# Compare empirical CDF to Phi at z = 0.907
print(f"Empirical P(Z > 0.907) = {(Z > 0.907).mean():.4f}")
print(f"CLT approximation      = {1 - stats.norm.cdf(0.907):.4f}")
