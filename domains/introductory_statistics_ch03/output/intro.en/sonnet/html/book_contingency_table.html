Concept Book: Contingency Table

Introductory Statistics Ch03 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A contingency table (also called a cross-tabulation or two-way frequency table) is a rectangular array that displays the joint frequency distribution of two or more categorical variables observed on the same collection of subjects. Formally, given categorical variables $X$ taking values in $\{x_1, \ldots, x_r\}$ and $Y$ taking values in $\{y_1, \ldots, y_c\}$, the contingency table is the matrix $\mathbf{N} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r \times c}$ whose $(i,j)$-entry is

$$n_{ij} = \#\{\text{observations with } X = x_i \text{ and } Y = y_j\}.$$

The marginal totals $n_{i\cdot} = \sum_j n_{ij}$ and $n_{\cdot j} = \sum_i n_{ij}$ encode the individual distributions, while the interior cells encode their interaction. The grand total $n = \sum_{i,j} n_{ij}$ normalises everything to empirical probabilities $\hat{p}_{ij} = n_{ij}/n$.

Worked example. Suppose 200 students are classified by study method ($X$: solo, group) and exam outcome ($Y$: pass, fail). The observed counts yield

$$\mathbf{N} = \begin{pmatrix} 90 & 30 \\ 40 & 40 \end{pmatrix}, \quad n = 200.$$

The expected count under independence is $e_{ij} = n_{i\cdot}\, n_{\cdot j}/n$; here $e_{11} = 130 \cdot 120/200 = 78$. The discrepancy between $\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{E}$ is the engine of all downstream inference.

Key theorem (Pearson, 1900). Under the null hypothesis of independence between $X$ and $Y$, the test statistic

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i,j} \frac{(n_{ij} - e_{ij})^2}{e_{ij}}$$

converges in distribution to $\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$ as $n \to \infty$. This single result transforms a table of counts into a rigorous decision about association.

The contingency table is the natural capstone of this course because every tool developed so far—set operations, conditional probability, the law of total probability, and estimation—converges here in one structure. Joint counts become joint probabilities; marginals become priors; the ratio $\hat{p}_{ij}/(\hat{p}_{i\cdot}\hat{p}_{\cdot j})$ measures the lift that one variable exerts on another, a quantity central to recommendation systems, epidemiology, and natural language processing alike. Independence is not merely a simplifying assumption; it is a testable, falsifiable claim, and the contingency table is the instrument that tests it.

Lab cell.

import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency
import sympy as sp

observed = [[90, 30], [40, 40]]
chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(observed)

chi2_sym, p_sym = sp.Rational(chi2).limit_denominator(1000), round(p, 4)
print(f"chi2 = {chi2:.4f}, p = {p:.4f}, dof = {dof}")
print("Expected counts:\n", expected)
