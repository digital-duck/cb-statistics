Concept Book: Tree Diagram

Introductory Statistics Ch03 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A tree diagram is a rooted, directed acyclic graph $T = (V, E)$ in which every vertex $v \in V$ except the root has exactly one incoming edge, and every edge is labelled with a transition value—most commonly a probability, a cost, or a multiplicity. Formally, if $r$ denotes the root and $\ell(v)$ denotes the label on the unique edge entering $v$, then the weight of any root-to-leaf path $\pi = (r = v_0, v_1, \ldots, v_k)$ is

$$w(\pi) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \ell(v_i).$$

This single multiplicative rule is the engine behind an extraordinary range of quantitative reasoning.

Worked example. A fair coin is tossed twice. The tree has root $\varnothing$, two children $\{H, T\}$ at depth 1, and four leaves $\{HH, HT, TH, TT\}$ at depth 2, each edge labelled $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The weight of the leaf $HT$ is $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{4}$, confirming the classical probability of that outcome. Summing all leaf weights gives $4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} = 1$, which verifies the axiom of total probability directly from the tree's structure.

Key theorem (Law of Total Probability via trees). Let $T$ be a probability tree whose leaves are exhaustive, mutually exclusive outcomes. Then

$$\sum_{\text{leaf } \lambda} w(\lambda) = 1,$$

and for any event $A$ expressible as a union of leaves, $P(A) = \sum_{\lambda \in A} w(\lambda)$. The proof follows by induction on tree depth: at depth 1 the branching probabilities sum to 1 by assumption, and the inductive step multiplies a subtree sum of 1 by the edge label entering its root.

Lab cell.

from sympy import Rational, symbols, product, pprint
from sympy.combinatorics import Permutation

p = Rational(1, 2)
paths = [('H','H'), ('H','T'), ('T','H'), ('T','T')]
weights = {path: p**len(path) for path in paths}
pprint(weights)
assert sum(weights.values()) == 1

The tree diagram is the natural endpoint of this concept-book because it externalises the chain rule of probability as a visible geometric object: multiplication becomes traversal, conditioning becomes branching, and independence becomes the absence of cross-edges between subtrees. Every topic covered earlier—sample spaces, conditional probability, independence, counting principles—finds its clearest expression as a structural property of this single diagram.
