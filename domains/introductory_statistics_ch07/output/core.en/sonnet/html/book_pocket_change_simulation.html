Concept Book: Pocket Change Simulation

Introductory Statistics Ch07 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A simulation is only as valuable as the breadth of phenomena it faithfully models. Throughout this chapter we have assembled every algebraic and probabilistic ingredient — discrete probability spaces, generating functions, recurrence relations, and expected-value calculus — and pocket_change_simulation is the machine into which all of those ingredients are fed simultaneously. It is not a single algorithm but a computational laboratory: given a finite set of coin denominations $\{d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k\} \subset \mathbb{Z}^+$ and a target amount $n$, the simulation samples uniformly at random from the complete set of additive compositions of $n$ over those denominations, accumulating empirical frequency counts that converge, by the Law of Large Numbers, to the exact combinatorial coefficients encoded in the generating function

$$G(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{1}{1 - x^{d_i}}.$$

Worked example. Take denominations $\{1, 5, 10\}$ and target $n = 15$. The coefficient of $x^{15}$ in $G(x)$ is $6$, corresponding to the six distinct multisets: $\{15 \times 1\}$, $\{10\times 1, 1\times 5\}$, $\{5\times 1, 2\times 5\}$, $\{3\times 5\}$, $\{5\times 1, 1\times 10\}$, $\{1\times 5, 1\times 10\}$. Running the simulation with $N = 10{,}000$ trials, each draw is produced by a random walk that greedily selects denominations with replacement until the running total reaches $n$ or is restarted; empirical relative frequencies stabilise around $1/6 \approx 0.1\overline{6}$ for each partition, confirming convergence.

Theorem (Convergence Rate). Let $\hat{p}_j^{(N)}$ denote the empirical frequency of partition $j$ after $N$ trials. By Hoeffding's inequality,

$$\Pr\!\left[\left|\hat{p}_j^{(N)} - p_j\right| > \varepsilon\right] \leq 2\exp\!\left(-2N\varepsilon^2\right),$$

so a precision of $\varepsilon = 0.01$ is achieved with probability at least $0.99$ after $N \geq \lceil \ln(200)/0.0002 \rceil = 26{,}492$ trials.

from sympy import symbols, series, Rational, prod

x = symbols('x')
denoms = [1, 5, 10]; n = 15
G = prod(1 / (1 - x**d) for d in denoms)
coeff = series(G, x, 0, n+1).coeff(x, n)
print(f"Exact partition count: {coeff}")   # → 6

The reach of this result extends far beyond loose change. The same generating-function skeleton governs inventory replenishment (denominations become reorder quantities), rhythmic composition in music theory (denominations become note durations that tile a measure), and knapsack cryptanalysis (where the absence of a clean generating-function factorisation is itself the security guarantee). Each domain inherits the convergence theorem and the SymPy workflow unchanged.
