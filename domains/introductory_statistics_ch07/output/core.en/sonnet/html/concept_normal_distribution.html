Normal Distribution

Definition. A continuous random variable $X$ is said to follow a normal distribution with mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ and variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, written $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, if its probability density function (PDF) is

$$f(x;\mu,\sigma^2) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad x \in \mathbb{R}.$$

The parameter $\mu$ controls the center of the symmetric bell-shaped curve, while $\sigma$ (the standard deviation) controls its spread. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) is $F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t;\mu,\sigma^2)\,dt$; because the integral has no closed form in elementary functions, probabilities are read from precomputed tables or computed numerically.

Worked Example. Suppose the heights of adult males in a population are modeled as $X \sim \mathcal{N}(175, 49)$ (centimeters), so $\mu = 175$ and $\sigma = 7$. Find the probability that a randomly selected male is taller than $182\,\text{cm}$.

Because every normal CDF can be expressed in terms of the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ table, set $z = (182 - 175)/7 = 1$ and look up $F(182) = F_{\mathcal{N}(0,1)}(1) \approx 0.8413$. Therefore

$$P(X > 182) = 1 - F(182) \approx 1 - 0.8413 = 0.1587.$$

Approximately $15.9\%$ of males exceed $182\,\text{cm}$.

Key Theorem (68–95–99.7 Rule). If $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$, then

$$P(\mu - k\sigma \le X \le \mu + k\sigma) \approx \begin{cases} 0.6827 & k=1,\\ 0.9545 & k=2,\\ 0.9973 & k=3. \end{cases}$$

This follows from evaluating $2F(\mu+k\sigma)-1$ at $k=1,2,3$, and it provides a rapid sanity-check for whether observed data are plausibly normal.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, exp, pi, sqrt, integrate, oo, simplify

x = symbols('x', real=True)
sigma_val = 7
mu_val = 175

f = (1 / (sigma_val * sqrt(2 * pi))) * exp(-(x - mu_val)**2 / (2 * sigma_val**2))

# P(X > 182)
prob = integrate(f, (x, 182, oo))
print("P(X > 182) =", simplify(prob))  # expects (1 - erf(1/sqrt(2))) / 2
