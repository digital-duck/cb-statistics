Concept Book: Pocket Change Simulation

Introductory Statistics Ch07 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the simulation achieves. Throughout this text we have built, layer by layer, the machinery needed to count, enumerate, and measure uncertainty. The pocket_change_simulation is the point at which those layers collapse into a single computational act: given a target amount $T$ and a set of denominations $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_k\}$, we draw independent random combinations of coins, record how often each combination achieves $T$, and thereby approximate the full probability distribution over the solution space. Formally, if $X$ denotes the number of ways to represent $T$ as an ordered multiset drawn from $\mathcal{D}$, the simulation estimates

$$\Pr\!\left[\text{combination sums to } T\right] \approx \frac{\#\{\text{successful trials}\}}{N}$$

where $N$ is the number of Monte Carlo iterations. This is not merely a counting exercise; it is the empirical counterpart to the analytic generating-function identity

$$\prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{1}{1 - x^{d_i}} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c(n)\, x^n,$$

where $c(n)$ is the exact number of representations of $n$. The simulation makes that coefficient tangible by constructing it from random walks in a discrete state space.

A worked illustration. Let $T = 10$ cents and $\mathcal{D} = \{1, 5, 10\}$. One trial selects coins uniformly at random until their sum meets or exceeds $T$, recording a success only on exact equality. After $N = 10{,}000$ trials, the empirical frequency converges to $c(10)/|\Omega|$, where $|\Omega|$ is the size of the sample space. Even for this small case the exact value $c(10) = 4$ is recovered to within statistical error $O(N^{-1/2})$, confirming the Law of Large Numbers in a setting every student has held in their hand.

The central convergence theorem. By the Strong Law, the simulated frequency $\hat{p}_N \to p$ almost surely as $N \to \infty$, and the Central Limit Theorem gives

$$\sqrt{N}\,(\hat{p}_N - p) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0,\, p(1-p)).$$

This guarantees that doubling precision costs four times the computation — a fundamental engineering trade-off the simulation makes visible.

Lab cell.

from sympy import symbols, Rational, summation, binomial
from collections import Counter
import random

def pocket_change_simulation(target, denoms, n_trials=10_000):
    successes = 0
    for _ in range(n_trials):
        total, bag = 0, []
        while total < target:
            coin = random.choice(denoms)
            bag.append(coin)
            total += coin
        if total == target:
            successes += 1
    return Rational(successes, n_trials)

print(pocket_change_simulation(10, [1, 5, 10]))
