Prediction Interpolation Extrapolation

Definition. Given a finite set of data points $\{(x_0, y_0), (x_1, y_1), \ldots, (x_n, y_n)\}$ with $x_0 < x_1 < \cdots < x_n$, interpolation is the process of estimating the value of an unknown function $f$ at a point $x^$ satisfying $x_0 \leq x^ \leq x_n$, while extrapolation refers to the analogous estimation at a point $x^$ lying outside this interval. Both tasks fall under the broader heading of prediction: using a model fitted to observed data to infer unobserved values. Formally, one constructs an approximant $\hat{f}$ such that $\hat{f}(x_i) = y_i$ for each $i$, then evaluates $\hat{f}(x^)$. The approximant $\hat{f}$ is typically a polynomial, spline, or regression model chosen to minimise some measure of error or complexity.

Worked Example. Suppose a laboratory measures the temperature $T$ (°C) of a chemical solution at three time points:

$$ (t_0, T_0) = (0,\, 20),\quad (t_1, T_1) = (2,\, 26),\quad (t_2, T_2) = (4,\, 30). $$

Constructing the unique interpolating polynomial of degree $\leq 2$ via the Lagrange formula:

$$ \hat{T}(t) = 20\cdot\frac{(t-2)(t-4)}{(0-2)(0-4)} + 26\cdot\frac{(t-0)(t-4)}{(2-0)(2-4)} + 30\cdot\frac{(t-0)(t-2)}{(4-0)(4-2)}. $$

Evaluating at $t^ = 3$ (interpolation) gives $\hat{T}(3) = 28.5\,°\text{C}$. Evaluating at $t^ = 6$ (extrapolation) gives $\hat{T}(6) = 32\,°\text{C}$; this value carries substantially greater uncertainty because no data constrain the model's behaviour beyond $t = 4$.

Key Theorem (Polynomial Interpolation Error). Let $f \in C^{n+1}[x_0, x_n]$ and let $p_n$ be the unique interpolating polynomial of degree $\leq n$ through the $n+1$ nodes. Then for each $x \in [x_0, x_n]$ there exists $\xi \in (x_0, x_n)$ such that

$$ f(x) - p_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\prod_{i=0}^{n}(x - x_i). $$

The product $\omega(x) = \prod_{i=0}^{n}(x - x_i)$ vanishes at every node and grows rapidly outside $[x_0, x_n]$, explaining why extrapolation error can diverge even when interpolation error remains small.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

import sympy as sp

t = sp.Symbol('t')
points = [(0, 20), (2, 26), (4, 30)]
poly = sp.interpolating_poly(len(points), t,
                             [p[0] for p in points],
                             [p[1] for p in points])
poly_simplified = sp.expand(poly)
print("Interpolating polynomial:", poly_simplified)
print("T(3) =", poly_simplified.subs(t, 3))   # interpolation
print("T(6) =", poly_simplified.subs(t, 6))   # extrapolation
