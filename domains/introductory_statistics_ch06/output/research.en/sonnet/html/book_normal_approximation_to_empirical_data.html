What the Normal Approximation Achieves

Throughout this textbook we have built, step by step, the machinery of descriptive statistics, probability distributions, and limit theorems. The concept of normal approximation to empirical data is the point at which that machinery becomes genuinely useful: it lets us replace a messy, finite collection of observations with a clean, analytically tractable model, and then reason about probabilities, confidence, and prediction with precision.

Formally, given a dataset $\{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\}$ with sample mean $\bar{x}$ and sample standard deviation $s$, the normal approximation asserts that the empirical distribution is well-described by the probability density

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{s\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x - \bar{x})^2}{2s^2}\right).$$

This is not merely a notational convenience. The Central Limit Theorem guarantees that, under mild conditions on the underlying population, the sampling distribution of $\bar{x}$ converges to a normal distribution as $n \to \infty$, making the approximation theoretically grounded rather than arbitrary.

Worked Example

Suppose a quality-control engineer records the mass (in grams) of $n = 120$ tablet capsules and obtains $\bar{x} = 500.3$ g and $s = 2.1$ g. The normal approximation models the mass of a randomly selected capsule as $X \sim \mathcal{N}(500.3,\, 2.1^2)$. The probability that a capsule falls within the specification window $[496, 504]$ g is

$$P(496 \le X \le 504) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{504 - 500.3}{2.1}\right) - \Phi\!\left(\frac{496 - 500.3}{2.1}\right) \approx \Phi(1.76) - \Phi(-2.05) \approx 0.939,$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the standard normal CDF. Without the normal approximation, this probability could only be estimated by counting; with it, a closed-form answer is available immediately.

Key Theorem

Theorem (Berry–Esseen, informal). Let $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be i.i.d. with mean $\mu$, variance $\sigma^2$, and finite third absolute moment $\rho = \mathbb{E}[|X - \mu|^3]$. Then the cumulative distribution of the standardized sample mean satisfies

$$\sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}} \left| F_n(t) - \Phi(t) \right| \le \frac{C\,\rho}{\sigma^3 \sqrt{n}},$$

where $C < 0.48$ is a universal constant. This bound makes the approximation error explicit and shrinking: doubling the sample size halves the worst-case error.

Lab Cell

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
import sympy as sp

# Symbolic verification of the probability calculation
x, mu, sigma = sp.symbols('x mu sigma', real=True)
pdf = (1 / (sigma * sp.sqrt(2 * sp.pi))) * sp.exp(-(x - mu)**2 / (2 * sigma**2))

# Numerical check with SciPy
mu_val, sigma_val = 500.3, 2.1
dist = stats.norm(loc=mu_val, scale=sigma_val)
prob = dist.cdf(504) - dist.cdf(496)
print(f"P(496 ≤ X ≤ 504) ≈ {prob:.4f}")   # Expected: ~0.9391

# Visualise the fit against simulated data
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
data = rng.normal(mu_val, sigma_val, size=120)
print(f"Empirical fraction in spec: {np.mean((data >= 496) & (data <= 504)):.4f}")
