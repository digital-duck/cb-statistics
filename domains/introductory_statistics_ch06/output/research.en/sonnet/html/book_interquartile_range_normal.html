The Interquartile Range of the Normal Distribution

Throughout this text we have built the machinery of probability densities, cumulative distribution functions, and quantile inversion. The interquartile range of the normal distribution draws all of these threads into a single, computable constant that bridges abstract theory and everyday measurement.

Definition. Let $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$. The first and third quartiles are $Q_1 = \mu + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(0.25)$ and $Q_3 = \mu + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(0.75)$, where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the quantile function of the standard normal. The interquartile range is

$$\mathrm{IQR}(X) = Q_3 - Q_1 = \sigma\bigl[\Phi^{-1}(0.75) - \Phi^{-1}(0.25)\bigr].$$

Because $\Phi^{-1}(0.75) \approx 0.6745$ and symmetry gives $\Phi^{-1}(0.25) = -\Phi^{-1}(0.75)$, the bracket collapses to the universal constant $2 \times 0.6745 \approx 1.3490$, so $\mathrm{IQR}(X) \approx 1.3490\,\sigma$.

Worked example. Adult human resting heart rates are well modelled by $\mathcal{N}(70, 10^2)$ beats per minute. Then $Q_1 = 70 - 6.745 \approx 63.3$ bpm and $Q_3 \approx 76.7$ bpm, giving $\mathrm{IQR} \approx 13.5$ bpm. The middle half of the healthy population thus spans roughly thirteen and a half beats — a clinically interpretable window that is unaffected by extreme outliers at either tail.

Key theorem (robust scale estimation). If $\hat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{IQR}} = \mathrm{IQR}_{\mathrm{sample}} / 1.3490$, then $\hat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{IQR}}$ is a consistent estimator of $\sigma$ under normality and retains meaningful interpretation when up to $25\%$ of observations are contaminated — a breakdown point inaccessible to the sample standard deviation.

Lab cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, sqrt, pi, exp, integrate, Rational, N
from sympy.stats import Normal, quantile

X = Normal('X', 0, 1)
Q1 = quantile(X)(Rational(1, 4))
Q3 = quantile(X)(Rational(3, 4))
IQR_constant = N(Q3 - Q1, 6)
print(IQR_constant)   # 1.34898

This constant is the natural endpoint of our journey: it is dimensionless, distribution-derived, and immediately actionable. Every concept developed in preceding chapters — integration of density functions, monotone transformation of random variables, and the symmetry of $\Phi$ — is necessary to arrive here and to prove why $1.3490$ is not a numerical accident but an inevitable consequence of Gaussian geometry.
