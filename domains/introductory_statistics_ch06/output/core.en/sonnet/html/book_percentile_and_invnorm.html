Concept Book: Percentile And Invnorm

Payoff

The journey through this textbook has built, layer by layer, the machinery needed to answer a deceptively simple question: given a probability, where does it land on the number line? The concept of percentile inversion via the normal quantile function — what we call percentile_and_invnorm — is the answer made precise, and it stands as the natural endpoint of introductory probability theory for exactly this reason: every earlier tool (expectation, variance, the bell curve, standardization) was moving toward the ability to invert cumulative probability into a concrete threshold.

Definition. Let $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$. The $p$-th percentile of $X$ is the value $x_p$ satisfying $P(X \le x_p) = p$, for $p \in (0,1)$. Because the standard normal CDF $\Phi$ is strictly increasing and continuous, its inverse $\Phi^{-1}$ exists everywhere on $(0,1)$. The general formula is

$$x_p = \mu + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(p).$$

This expresses the quantile as a location shift plus a scale-adjusted inverse normal, the quantity $\Phi^{-1}(p)$ being the $z$-score corresponding to cumulative probability $p$.

Worked example. Suppose adult resting heart rates follow $\mathcal{N}(70, 10^2)$ beats per minute. A cardiologist flags the top $5\%$ of patients for monitoring. The threshold is

$$x_{0.95} = 70 + 10\,\Phi^{-1}(0.95) = 70 + 10(1.6449) \approx 86.4 \text{ bpm}.$$

Any patient above $86.4$ bpm falls in the clinical-alert percentile — a number extracted from pure probability.

Key theorem (Quantile equivariance). If $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$ and $Y = aX + b$ with $a > 0$, then the $p$-th percentile of $Y$ equals $a\,x_p + b$. Consequently, percentile calculations are closed under affine transformation, a property that makes standardization reversible and the formula $x_p = \mu + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(p)$ exact rather than approximate.

from sympy import symbols, N
from sympy.stats import Normal, quantile

mu, sigma = 70, 10
X = Normal('X', mu, sigma)
p = 0.95
threshold = quantile(X, p)
print(f"95th-percentile threshold: {N(threshold, 4)} bpm")

Connection to interquartile range. The immediate application waiting at the door is the interquartile range under normality. The IQR is nothing more than two percentile inversions at $p = 0.75$ and $p = 0.25$:

$$\text{IQR} = x_{0.75} - x_{0.25} = \sigma\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(0.75) - \Phi^{-1}(0.25)\bigr) \approx 1.3490\,\sigma.$$

This single identity connects the spread of a dataset — a statistic any analyst can compute — to the theoretical standard deviation, enabling robust estimation and outlier detection grounded in the same normal model built throughout this text.
