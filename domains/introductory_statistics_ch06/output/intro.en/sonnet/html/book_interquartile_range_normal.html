Concept Book: Interquartile Range Normal

Introductory Statistics Ch06 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Throughout this text, we have built the machinery of probability density, cumulative distribution, and quantile inversion. The interquartile range of the normal distribution is the point at which these threads converge into a single, practically powerful quantity.

Definition. For a random variable $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the interquartile range is

$$\mathrm{IQR}(X) = Q_3 - Q_1 = F^{-1}(0.75) - F^{-1}(0.25),$$

where $F^{-1}$ denotes the quantile function (inverse CDF). Because $X = \mu + \sigma Z$ with $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, linearity of quantiles gives

$$\mathrm{IQR}(X) = \sigma\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(0.75) - \Phi^{-1}(0.25)\bigr) \approx 1.3490\,\sigma.$$

The constant $1.3490$ is exact up to rounding: $2\,\Phi^{-1}(0.75) \approx 2 \times 0.6745$. This proportionality is the central result.

Worked Example. Suppose exam scores are modelled as $X \sim \mathcal{N}(72, 10^2)$. Then

$$Q_1 = 72 - 0.6745 \times 10 = 65.26, \qquad Q_3 = 72 + 0.6745 \times 10 = 78.74,$$

so $\mathrm{IQR} = 13.49$. Exactly half the cohort scores between $65.26$ and $78.74$, a statement that requires neither the mean nor the variance to verify in real data — only rank order.

Key Theorem (Robust Scale Estimation). Define the normalised IQR estimator $\hat{\sigma} = \widehat{\mathrm{IQR}}/1.3490$. Under the normal model, $\hat{\sigma}$ is a consistent, Fisher-efficient estimator of $\sigma$ that achieves a breakdown point of $25\%$: up to one quarter of observations may be arbitrarily corrupted without pushing $\hat{\sigma}$ to infinity. By contrast, the sample standard deviation has breakdown point $0\%$ — a single outlier suffices to destabilise it.

from sympy import symbols, sqrt, pi, exp, integrate, Rational, N
from sympy.stats import Normal, cdf

sigma = symbols('sigma', positive=True)
X = Normal('X', 0, sigma)
q3 = cdf(X).rewrite('erf').subs('x', sigma)   # illustrative
iqr_coeff = N(2 * 0.6744897501960817, 10)
print(f"IQR = {iqr_coeff} * sigma")

The identity $\mathrm{IQR} = 1.3490\,\sigma$ is the natural endpoint of this book because it translates an abstract parameter — the standard deviation — into a quantity measurable from any ordered dataset, no arithmetic mean required. It is the bridge between the Gaussian ideal and the messy empirical world.

That bridge carries real traffic. In clinical trials, the IQR appears in CONSORT-mandated summary tables precisely because trial populations frequently include outlying responders. In manufacturing quality control, a process is declared capable when its $\hat{\sigma}_{\mathrm{IQR}}$ keeps six estimated standard deviations inside specification limits. In exploratory data analysis, Tukey's box-and-whisker fence — $Q_1 - 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$ to $Q_3 + 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$ — flags roughly $0.7\%$ of normal observations as potential outliers, a threshold derived directly from the formula above.

Each of these domains rewards deeper study. If you wish to follow one thread further, turn to the chapter on robust estimation: there you will meet the median absolute deviation, Huber's $M$-estimator, and the full minimax theory that explains why $25\%$ is the exact breakdown frontier — and why the interquartile range sits precisely at the heart of it.
