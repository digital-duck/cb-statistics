Concept Book: Randomized Experiment

Introductory Statistics Ch01 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A randomized experiment is the logical destination of every analytical tool assembled in this text. Formally, a randomized experiment is a study in which each unit $i$ drawn from a population $\mathcal{U}$ is assigned to treatment $T_i \in \{0, 1\}$ by a known, investigator-controlled probability mechanism — most commonly independent Bernoulli draws with $P(T_i = 1) = p$ for some fixed $p \in (0,1)$. The defining property is that assignment is independent of all covariates, observed or latent: $T_i \perp (Y_i(0), Y_i(1), \mathbf{X}_i)$, where $Y_i(0)$ and $Y_i(1)$ denote the potential outcomes under control and treatment respectively (the Rubin causal model).

Why randomization is the natural endpoint. Every concept introduced earlier — probability spaces, expectation, hypothesis testing, regression — was a partial answer to the question how do we separate signal from confounding? Randomization answers it completely. Because $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_i \mid T_i = 1] = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_i \mid T_i = 0]$ in expectation over repeated assignments, the simple difference-in-means estimator

$$\hat{\tau} = \frac{1}{n_1}\sum_{T_i=1} Y_i - \frac{1}{n_0}\sum_{T_i=0} Y_i$$

is unbiased for the average treatment effect $\tau = \mathbb{E}[Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)]$ without any regression adjustment. No observational design achieves this guarantee unconditionally.

Worked example. Suppose a university tests whether a supplemental tutoring session raises exam scores. Assign $n = 60$ students by coin flip ($p = 0.5$). Treated mean: $\bar{Y}_1 = 82$; control mean: $\bar{Y}_0 = 75$; pooled standard deviation $s = 10$. The two-sample $t$-statistic is

$$t = \frac{82 - 75}{10\sqrt{1/30 + 1/30}} = \frac{7}{2.58} \approx 2.71, \quad p \approx 0.008.$$

Because assignment was random, we conclude the tutoring caused the 7-point gain — a causal claim that a correlational study could never license.

Connection to Research Ethics and the IRB. This causal power is precisely why randomized experiments are subject to Institutional Review Board (IRB) oversight. When an investigator controls $T_i$, they bear direct responsibility for any harm the treatment inflicts. The ethical framework — informed consent, equipoise, stopping rules, and data-safety monitoring — is the governance layer that makes randomized experimentation morally permissible. Understanding the statistical structure of an experiment is therefore inseparable from understanding the ethical obligations it generates; the IRB protocol is, in a precise sense, a formal description of the probability mechanism and its boundaries.

import sympy as sp

n1, n0, y1, y0, s = 30, 30, 82, 75, 10
se = s * sp.sqrt(sp.Rational(1, n1) + sp.Rational(1, n0))
t_stat = (y1 - y0) / se
