Data Collection Design

Definition. A data collection design is a formal specification of the sampling procedure, measurement protocol, and assignment mechanism used to gather observations from a population. Formally, let $\Omega$ be a population of units and let $\mathcal{X}$ be a measurable outcome space. A design $\mathcal{D}$ consists of a triple $(\mathcal{S}, \pi, \mathbf{M})$, where $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \Omega$ is the selected sample, $\pi: \Omega \to (0,1]$ is the inclusion probability function satisfying $\sum_{u \in \Omega} \pi(u) \geq |\mathcal{S}|$, and $\mathbf{M}$ is a measurement operator mapping each unit $u \in \mathcal{S}$ to an observed value $x_u \in \mathcal{X}$. The design is called probabilistic if every unit has a strictly positive inclusion probability, a requirement that enables unbiased estimation.

Worked Example. A researcher wishes to estimate the mean exam score $\mu$ across $N = 1{,}000$ students. Under simple random sampling without replacement, each subset of size $n = 100$ is equally likely, so every unit shares the common inclusion probability $\pi(u) = n/N = 0.1$. The sample mean $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$ is an unbiased estimator of $\mu$, and its variance is

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar{x}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\left(1 - \frac{n}{N}\right),$$

where $\sigma^2$ is the population variance and the factor $(1 - n/N)$ is the finite population correction. With $\sigma = 15$ and $n/N = 0.1$, we obtain $\operatorname{Var}(\bar{x}) = 225/100 \times 0.9 = 2.025$, giving a standard error of approximately $1.42$ points.

Key Theorem (Design-Based Unbiasedness). Let $\hat{\tau} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{S}} x_u / \pi(u)$ be any estimator of the population total $\tau = \sum_{u \in \Omega} x_u$ constructed by weighting each observed value by the reciprocal of its inclusion probability. Under any probabilistic design, $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\tau}] = \tau$. The proof follows by linearity of expectation and the identity $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{u \in \mathcal{S}}] = \pi(u)$, which yields $\mathbb{E}[x_u / \pi(u)] = x_u$ for each unit. This theorem guarantees design-based unbiasedness regardless of any distributional assumptions about $x_u$; unbiasedness is a property of the design $\mathcal{D}$, not of the data-generating model.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
from sympy import symbols, Rational, simplify

n, N, sigma2 = symbols('n N sigma2', positive=True)

# Variance of sample mean under simple random sampling without replacement
var_xbar = (sigma2 / n) * (1 - n / N)
print("Var(x̄) =", simplify(var_xbar))

# Numerical evaluation
vals = {n: 100, N: 1000, sigma2: 225}
print("Numeric SE =", float(var_xbar.subs(vals)**Rational(1,2)))

Running this cell confirms the analytical result: the standard error is $\approx 1.42$, illustrating how the design parameters $n$ and $N$ jointly control estimation precision.
