Research Ethics Irb

Definition. An Institutional Review Board (IRB) is a formally designated committee, mandated under federal regulation (45 CFR §46, the "Common Rule" in the United States), that reviews, approves, and monitors research involving human subjects. Its core mandate is to ensure that three foundational principles—articulated in the 1979 Belmont Report—are upheld: respect for persons (autonomy and informed consent), beneficence (maximizing benefit and minimizing harm), and justice (equitable distribution of research burdens and benefits). No federally funded research involving human participants may commence without prior IRB authorization.

Research protocols are classified by risk level. Exempt research poses minimal risk and involves only standard educational practices or anonymous surveys. Expedited review applies to research involving no more than minimal risk but not qualifying as exempt—such as collection of voice recordings or moderate exercise studies. Full board review is required whenever research involves greater than minimal risk, vulnerable populations (minors, prisoners, pregnant women), deception, or identifiable sensitive data.

Worked Example. Suppose a researcher designs a survey studying students' academic stress levels using a validated psychological instrument. Participants are adults (age ≥ 18), responses are collected anonymously via an encrypted platform, and no deception is involved. The risk profile is minimal: no physical procedures, no sensitive identifiers, and standard educational measurement tools. This protocol qualifies for expedited review. The researcher submits: (1) a study protocol describing procedures, (2) the survey instrument, (3) an informed consent form disclosing purpose, voluntary participation, and data handling, and (4) a data security plan. The IRB officer reviews and approves within 10 business days.

Key Theorem (Minimal Risk Standard). Research is classified as minimal risk if the probability and magnitude of harm anticipated in the study are not greater than those ordinarily encountered in daily life or during routine physical or psychological examinations. Formally, let $H$ denote the harm random variable for a participant. The condition is:

$$\Pr(H > h_0) \leq \Pr(H_{\text{daily}} > h_0) \quad \forall\, h_0 > 0$$

where $H_{\text{daily}}$ represents the harm distribution of ordinary daily experience.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import symbols, Piecewise

r = symbols('r')  # risk level: 0 = exempt, 1 = expedited, 2 = full board
review_type = Piecewise(
    ("Exempt",     r < 1),
    ("Expedited",  r < 2),
    ("Full Board", True)
)
for level in [0, 1, 2]:
    print(f"Risk {level}: {review_type.subs(r, level)}")
