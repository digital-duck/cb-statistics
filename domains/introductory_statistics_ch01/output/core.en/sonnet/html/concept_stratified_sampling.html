Stratified Sampling

Definition. Let $\Omega$ be a finite or infinite population partitioned into $K$ mutually exclusive, exhaustive subgroups called strata, $\Omega = S_1 \cup S_2 \cup \cdots \cup S_K$, with $S_i \cap S_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$. Denote the relative size (weight) of stratum $k$ by $w_k = |S_k|/|\Omega|$, so $\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k = 1$. A stratified random sample of total size $n$ draws $n_k$ units independently and uniformly at random from $S_k$, where $\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_k = n$. The proportional allocation rule sets $n_k = \lfloor w_k n \rfloor$, ensuring each stratum is represented in proportion to its population share.

Worked Example. A university has 10 000 students: 6 000 undergraduates, 3 000 postgraduates, and 1 000 staff. A researcher wishes to survey $n = 100$ individuals about library usage. Under proportional allocation:

$$n_{\text{ug}} = \left\lfloor 0.60 \times 100 \right\rfloor = 60, \quad n_{\text{pg}} = 30, \quad n_{\text{staff}} = 10.$$

Each group is sampled independently. The overall sample mean is the weighted combination

$$\bar{X}_{\text{strat}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \bar{X}_k,$$

where $\bar{X}_k$ is the sample mean within stratum $k$.

Key Theorem (Variance Reduction). Let $\sigma_k^2$ denote the within-stratum population variance and $\bar{X}_{\text{SRS}}$ the mean of a simple random sample of the same size $n$. Then

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_{\text{strat}}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{w_k^2 \sigma_k^2}{n_k} \leq \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}_{\text{SRS}}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n},$$

with equality only when all stratum means are identical. The gain is largest when strata are internally homogeneous but differ markedly from one another — precisely the condition the partition is designed to exploit.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
from sympy import symbols, Rational, sqrt, simplify

# Population: three strata
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
strata = {
    'ug':    rng.normal(loc=3.5, scale=0.4, size=6000),
    'pg':    rng.normal(loc=4.2, scale=0.3, size=3000),
    'staff': rng.normal(loc=4.8, scale=0.2, size=1000),
}
weights = {'ug': 0.6, 'pg': 0.3, 'staff': 0.1}
n_k     = {'ug': 60,  'pg': 30,  'staff': 10}

# Stratified estimate
strat_means = {k: rng.choice(v, n_k[k], replace=False).mean()
               for k, v in strata.items()}
strat_estimate = sum(weights[k] * strat_means[k] for k in weights)

# Simple random sample of same total size
pool = np.concatenate(list(strata.values()))
srs_estimate = rng.choice(pool, 100, replace=False).mean()

print(f"Stratified mean : {strat_estimate:.4f}")
print(f"SRS mean        : {srs_estimate:.4f}")
print(f"True population : {sum(weights[k]*v.mean() for k,v in strata.items()):.4f}")

Running this cell repeatedly will demonstrate that strat_estimate clusters more tightly around the true mean than srs_estimate, providing empirical confirmation of the variance-reduction theorem above.
