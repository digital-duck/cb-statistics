Simple Random Sampling

Definition. Let $U = \{u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_N\}$ be a finite population of size $N$. A simple random sample of size $n$ (SRS) is a subset $S \subseteq U$ of exactly $n$ distinct elements chosen such that every possible subset of size $n$ has an equal probability of being selected. The number of such subsets is $\binom{N}{n}$, so each particular sample $S$ satisfies

$$\Pr(S) = \frac{1}{\dbinom{N}{n}}.$$

An equivalent characterization is that, at each draw, every element remaining in the population is equally likely to be selected, giving a sequential selection probability of $\frac{1}{N}$, $\frac{1}{N-1}$, $\ldots$, $\frac{1}{N-n+1}$ at successive steps.

Worked Example. A quality-control engineer inspects a shipment of $N = 200$ circuit boards by drawing $n = 5$ at random. The probability that any specific set of five boards is selected is

$$\Pr(S) = \frac{1}{\binom{200}{5}} = \frac{1}{2{,}535{,}650{,}040} \approx 3.94 \times 10^{-10}.$$

Of the 200 boards, suppose 20 are defective. The probability that the sample contains at least one defective board is computed via its complement:

$$\Pr(\text{at least one defective}) = 1 - \frac{\binom{180}{5}}{\binom{200}{5}} = 1 - \frac{1{,}458{,}894{,}060}{2{,}535{,}650{,}040} \approx 0.425.$$

Thus there is roughly a 42.5 % chance of detecting the fault in a single sample.

Key Theorem (Unbiasedness of the Sample Mean). Let $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ denote the values of a study variable observed in an SRS. The sample mean $\bar{y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n y_i$ is an unbiased estimator of the population mean $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i$, i.e., $\mathbb{E}[\bar{y}] = \bar{Y}$. Its variance is

$$\mathrm{Var}(\bar{y}) = \frac{S^2}{n}\left(1 - \frac{n}{N}\right), \quad S^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2,$$

where the factor $(1 - n/N)$ accounts for the reduction in variance that arises when a non-negligible fraction of the population is sampled without replacement.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import binomial, Rational, N as sym_N

pop, sample, defective = 200, 5, 20
good = pop - defective

prob_none_defective = Rational(binomial(good, sample), binomial(pop, sample))
prob_at_least_one   = 1 - prob_none_defective

print("P(no defective) =", sym_N(prob_none_defective, 6))
print("P(≥1 defective) =", sym_N(prob_at_least_one,  6))

Running this cell reproduces the exact rational probability before converting to a decimal, illustrating SymPy's ability to handle combinatorially large exact integers without floating-point rounding.
