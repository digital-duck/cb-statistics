What Research Ethics and the IRB Framework Achieve

An Institutional Review Board (IRB) is a formally constituted committee charged with prospective, independent review of research protocols involving human subjects. Its mandate derives from the principle of respect for persons, codified in the Belmont Report (1979) as three interlocking norms: respect for autonomy, beneficence, and justice. Formally, let $\mathcal{P}$ denote the population of prospective participants, $\mathcal{R}$ a research protocol, and $\mathcal{H}: \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{P} \to \mathbb{R}$ a harm functional. IRB approval constitutes a certified bound

$$\sup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \mathcal{H}(\mathcal{R}, p) \leq \epsilon,$$

where $\epsilon$ is a socially negotiated threshold of acceptable risk, weighed against projected benefit $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{R})$ satisfying $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{R}) \gg \epsilon$.

Worked Example

Consider a survey study measuring mathematical anxiety in undergraduates. The researcher's protocol specifies anonymous response collection, voluntary participation with the right to withdraw at any time, and aggregate-only reporting. An IRB review maps each protocol element to a Belmont principle: anonymization satisfies confidentiality (autonomy); aggregate reporting limits stigma risk (beneficence); sampling across all majors, not only STEM, prevents systematic exclusion of vulnerable subgroups (justice). The board certifies the bound above and issues an approval number before any data collection begins.

Key Theorem (Informed Consent Completeness)

Theorem. A consent process is complete if and only if a rational participant, given the disclosed information $\mathcal{I}$, can form an unbiased posterior estimate of personal risk $\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{R}, p)$ and expected benefit $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{R}, p)$. Incompleteness — omitting material risks or misrepresenting procedures — renders downstream data ethically inadmissible regardless of scientific merit. This result formalizes why protocol deception (even benign) requires explicit IRB justification and mandatory debriefing.

Why This Is the Natural Endpoint

Every preceding chapter constructed analytical tools — sampling distributions, regression, causal inference, and hypothesis testing — that are powerful precisely because they operate on human-generated data. Research ethics is the capstone because it supplies the legitimacy condition without which those tools have no valid domain of application. A model is only as trustworthy as the consent of the people who generated its training set.
