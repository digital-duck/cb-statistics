Concept Book: Randomized Experiment

Introductory Statistics Ch01 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A randomized experiment is the methodological apex of empirical inquiry: a study design in which units are assigned to treatment conditions by a known, investigator-controlled probability mechanism rather than by self-selection, convenience, or historical accident. Formally, let $\mathcal{U} = \{u_1, \dots, u_n\}$ be a finite population of experimental units and let $T : \mathcal{U} \to \{0, 1\}$ be a treatment indicator. An experiment is randomized if $T$ is drawn from a probability distribution $\mathbb{P}$ that is fully specified before data collection and is independent of each unit's potential outcomes $\bigl(Y_i(0), Y_i(1)\bigr)$. The canonical example is simple random assignment: each unit is independently assigned to treatment with probability $\pi \in (0,1)$, so $T_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi)$ i.i.d.

This mechanism earns its privileged status through a single, decisive theorem.

Theorem (Unbiasedness of the Difference-in-Means Estimator). Under simple random assignment with $\pi = \tfrac{1}{2}$, the estimator

$$\hat{\tau} = \frac{1}{n_1}\sum_{i: T_i=1} Y_i - \frac{1}{n_0}\sum_{i: T_i=0} Y_i$$

satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\tau}] = \tau := \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\bigr]$, the average treatment effect (ATE).

Proof sketch. Because $T_i \perp \!\!\! \perp \bigl(Y_i(0), Y_i(1)\bigr)$ by design, $\mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid T_i = 1] = \mathbb{E}[Y_i(1)]$ and $\mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid T_i = 0] = \mathbb{E}[Y_i(0)]$. The result follows by linearity of expectation. $\square$

No analogous guarantee holds for observational data, because unobserved confounders can induce dependence between $T$ and the potential outcomes, rendering $\hat{\tau}$ arbitrarily biased.

Worked example. A university tests two study-skills curricula on $n = 60$ students. Coins are flipped independently to assign 30 students to each curriculum. End-of-term scores are recorded. The difference-in-means $\hat{\tau} = 78.4 - 74.1 = 4.3$ points estimates the ATE without adjustment for prior GPA, because randomization has balanced that covariate in expectation.

import numpy as np
from sympy import symbols, Rational, simplify

# Symbolic verification: E[Y | T=1] - E[T=0] under random assignment
Y0, Y1, pi = symbols('Y0 Y1 pi', real=True)
E_treated   = Y1          # independence collapses conditional to marginal
E_control   = Y0
ATE_hat     = simplify(E_treated - E_control)
print("Estimated ATE:", ATE_hat)   # Y1 - Y0 = tau, as claimed

The reach of randomized experiments extends immediately into research ethics and Institutional Review Board (IRB) practice. An IRB evaluates whether a proposed experiment exposes human participants to undue risk relative to anticipated benefit — a calculation that is coherent only when the experiment can plausibly produce valid causal evidence. The randomization mechanism is not merely a statistical convenience; it is an ethical commitment: it ensures that no participant is systematically disadvantaged by a confounded allocation rule, and it makes the study's inferential claims auditable by third parties. Requirements such as equipoise (genuine uncertainty about which arm is superior), pre-registration of the primary endpoint, and stopping rules for harm monitoring all presuppose that the study is randomized and that its probability model is specified in advance.

The connection is therefore structural, not incidental. A randomized design makes the IRB's cost–benefit analysis tractable; the IRB's oversight makes the randomized design ethically permissible. Each institution sustains the other.
