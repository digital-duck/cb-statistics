Payoff

The preceding sections built the formal machinery of experimental design, statistical inference, and causal identification. Every estimator, every hypothesis test, every confidence interval derived in this book rests on an implicit premise: the data were collected under conditions that permit valid scientific inference and respect the persons whose information was recorded. Research ethics, as operationalized through Institutional Review Board (IRB) oversight, is the mechanism that enforces this premise. It is not an administrative afterthought; it is the logical closure of the scientific method as applied to human subjects.

What the IRB Framework Achieves. An IRB is an independent committee empowered to review, approve, modify, or prohibit research involving human participants. Its mandate derives from the Belmont Report (1979), which distilled centuries of painful lessons — from Nuremberg to Tuskegee — into three axiomatic principles: respect for persons (autonomy and informed consent), beneficence (maximizing benefit while minimizing harm), and justice (equitable distribution of research burdens and benefits). Formally, let $\mathcal{R}$ denote a research protocol, $\mathcal{P}$ the participant population, and $\mathcal{H}$ the measurable harm space. The IRB approval condition can be stated schematically as

$$\text{Approve}(\mathcal{R}) \iff \mathbb{E}[\text{Benefit}(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{P})] - \mathbb{E}[\text{Harm}(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{P})] > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Consent}(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{P}) = \top.$$

This is not a loose heuristic; it is a structured decision procedure that mirrors the cost-benefit analysis you applied when choosing between experimental designs in Chapter 4.

Why It Is the Natural Endpoint. Every concept introduced in this book — randomization, blinding, power calculation, multiple-comparison correction — was developed in service of reliable inference. Reliable inference, however, is only valuable if the underlying data collection was ethically defensible. A study with perfect internal validity but coerced participants produces knowledge that cannot be used, published, or replicated within any legitimate scientific community. The IRB framework thus acts as a completeness condition: it certifies that the technical apparatus you have mastered is deployed in a domain where its outputs carry moral as well as epistemic weight.

An Invitation. Of all the domains where these principles converge most acutely, clinical trials stand out: randomization determines who receives an experimental therapy, power calculations govern how long participants remain exposed to risk, and interim analyses must balance the statistician's desire for more data against the ethicist's obligation to stop early when harm is evident. The next chapter takes one such trial — a Phase II oncology study — and traces every technical decision made in this book back to the IRB protocol that authorized it. Follow that thread, and the distinction between statistical methodology and research ethics will dissolve into a single, unified practice.
