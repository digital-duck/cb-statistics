Randomized Experiment

Definition. A randomized experiment (also called a randomized controlled trial) is a study in which the investigator assigns each experimental unit to a treatment condition via a random mechanism—typically drawing from a uniform distribution over treatment labels—independently of any characteristics of the unit. Formally, let $\mathcal{U} = \{u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_n\}$ be a finite population of units, and let $\mathcal{T} = \{0, 1\}$ denote a binary treatment set. A completely randomized design assigns treatment $T_i \in \mathcal{T}$ to unit $u_i$ such that

$$\Pr(T_i = 1) = p \in (0,1), \quad T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_n \text{ mutually independent.}$$

The defining property is that treatment assignment $T_i$ is statistically independent of all pre-treatment covariates, both measured and unmeasured. This independence is the mechanism that justifies causal inference.

Worked Example. Suppose a university evaluates a tutoring programme for $n = 100$ students. Each student is assigned by a coin flip ($p = 0.5$) to either tutoring ($T_i = 1$) or control ($T_i = 0$). After the semester, exam scores $Y_i$ are recorded. The sample average treatment effect (SATE) is estimated by

$$\widehat{\tau} = \bar{Y}_1 - \bar{Y}_0 = \frac{1}{n_1}\sum_{T_i=1} Y_i \;-\; \frac{1}{n_0}\sum_{T_i=0} Y_i,$$

where $n_1 + n_0 = 100$. Because assignment was random, differences in $\bar{Y}_1$ and $\bar{Y}_0$ cannot be attributed to pre-existing ability gaps; they reflect the causal effect of tutoring.

Key Theorem (Unbiasedness of the Difference-in-Means Estimator). Under a completely randomized design with $n_1$ units assigned to treatment and $n_0$ to control,

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{\tau}\right] = \tau_{\text{ATE}} := \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\!\left(Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)\right),$$

where $Y_i(t)$ denotes unit $i$'s potential outcome under treatment $t$. The proof follows from the exchangeability induced by random assignment: $\mathbb{E}[\bar{Y}_1] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i Y_i(1)$ and similarly for control.

Lab Cell.

import numpy as np
from sympy import symbols, Rational, simplify

# Simulate a small randomized experiment
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
n = 100
treatment = rng.binomial(1, 0.5, size=n)
Y0 = rng.normal(70, 10, size=n)          # potential outcome: control
Y1 = Y0 + 5                               # true effect = 5 points
Y_obs = np.where(treatment, Y1, Y0)

tau_hat = Y_obs[treatment == 1].mean() - Y_obs[treatment == 0].mean()
print(f"Estimated ATE: {tau_hat:.3f}  (true ATE = 5.000)")

Running this cell repeatedly demonstrates that $\widehat{\tau}$ fluctuates around the true value of $5$, confirming unbiasedness empirically and motivating the study of variance reduction through blocking and stratification.
