Concept Book: Empirical Vs Theoretical Distribution Comparison

Introductory Statistics Ch05 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Empirical vs. Theoretical Distribution Comparison is the act of measuring, in a precise statistical sense, how well a proposed probability model $F_\theta$ accounts for data drawn from an unknown population. Given $n$ independent observations $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, the empirical cumulative distribution function is

$$\hat{F}_n(x) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}[x_i \le x],$$

while the theoretical CDF under the assumed model is $F_\theta(x)$. The comparison asks: is the discrepancy $\sup_x |\hat{F}_n(x) - F_\theta(x)|$ consistent with sampling variation, or does it reveal a genuine misfit?

Worked example. Suppose a quality-control engineer records 200 component lifetimes and hypothesises that failures follow an Exponential distribution with rate $\hat{\lambda} = 1/\bar{x}$. She plots $\hat{F}_n$ alongside $F_{\hat{\lambda}}(x) = 1 - e^{-\hat{\lambda}x}$ and computes the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic $D_{200}$. At the $\alpha = 0.05$ level the critical value is $c \approx 0.096$; if $D_{200} > c$ she rejects the Exponential model and investigates whether a Weibull distribution—which allows a non-constant hazard rate—fits better.

Key theorem (Glivenko–Cantelli). For any continuous CDF $F$,

$$\sup_x |\hat{F}_n(x) - F(x)| \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

This guarantees that $\hat{F}_n$ is a consistent estimator of the true CDF regardless of its shape, making the comparison asymptotically meaningful. The Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz inequality further provides the non-asymptotic bound $P\!\left(\sup_x|\hat{F}_n(x)-F(x)|>\varepsilon\right)\le 2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}$, which is distribution-free and immediately actionable in finite samples.

Lab cell (SymPy / SciPy).

from scipy import stats
import numpy as np

rng  = np.random.default_rng(42)
data = rng.exponential(scale=5, size=200)        # synthetic lifetimes
lam_hat = 1 / data.mean()
D, p    = stats.kstest(data, "expon", args=(0, 1/lam_hat))
print(f"KS statistic D = {D:.4f},  p-value = {p:.4f}")

This concept is the natural endpoint of a probabilistic curriculum because every earlier idea—random variables, moments, parametric families, maximum-likelihood estimation—converges here into a single, falsifiable claim about the real world. Confirming or rejecting that claim is not a formality; it is the scientific act. The techniques extend directly to hypothesis testing, model selection via AIC/BIC, simulation validation, and goodness-of-fit diagnostics in machine learning.
