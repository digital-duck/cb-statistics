What One-Way ANOVA Achieves

Throughout this text we have built, layer by layer, the machinery of statistical inference: probability distributions, the sampling distribution of the mean, the $t$-test for two populations, and the $F$-distribution. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) is the structure that unifies these ideas into a single, principled answer to a question that two-sample methods cannot cleanly address: do the means of $k \geq 2$ independent groups differ beyond what chance alone would produce?

Formally, given $k$ independent random samples $\{x_{ij}\}$ with $i = 1, \ldots, k$ and $j = 1, \ldots, n_i$, one-way ANOVA partitions the total sum of squares

$$SS_{\text{Total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}(x_{ij} - \bar{x}_{..})^2$$

into a between-groups component $SS_{\text{Between}}$ and a within-groups component $SS_{\text{Within}}$, so that $SS_{\text{Total}} = SS_{\text{Between}} + SS_{\text{Within}}$. The test statistic

$$F = \frac{MS_{\text{Between}}}{MS_{\text{Within}}} = \frac{SS_{\text{Between}}/(k-1)}{SS_{\text{Within}}/(N-k)}$$

follows an $F(k-1,\, N-k)$ distribution under the null hypothesis $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$, assuming normality and homoscedasticity within each group.

Worked Example

Suppose an agronomist measures crop yield (kg per plot) under three fertiliser regimes ($k = 3$, $n_i = 5$ each). She records group means $\bar{x}_{1.} = 12.4$, $\bar{x}_{2.} = 15.1$, $\bar{x}_{3.} = 11.8$ and grand mean $\bar{x}_{..} = 13.1$. Computing the mean squares yields $MS_{\text{Between}} = 23.7$ and $MS_{\text{Within}} = 4.2$, giving $F = 5.64$. The critical value $F_{0.05}(2, 12) \approx 3.89$; since $5.64 > 3.89$, she rejects $H_0$ and concludes that at least one fertiliser mean differs significantly.

Key Theorem

ANOVA Decomposition Theorem. Under the Gauss–Markov assumptions for the fixed-effects model $x_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \varepsilon_{ij}$, the statistics $SS_{\text{Between}}/\sigma^2$ and $SS_{\text{Within}}/\sigma^2$ are independently $\chi^2$-distributed with $k-1$ and $N-k$ degrees of freedom, respectively. Their ratio therefore follows an exact $F$-distribution, guaranteeing valid Type-I error control at any pre-specified significance level $\alpha$.

Lab Cell

from sympy.stats import F, P, cdf
from sympy import symbols, N

F_stat = 5.64          # observed test statistic
df1, df2 = 2, 12       # degrees of freedom

x = symbols('x', positive=True)
F_dist = F('F', df1, df2)

p_value = float(1 - cdf(F_dist)(F_stat))
print(f"p-value ≈ {p_value:.4f}")   # expected: ≈ 0.019

Running this cell confirms the agronomist's rejection of $H_0$ at $\alpha = 0.05$, closing the loop between pencil-and-paper calculation and symbolic computation.

The Natural Endpoint

One-way ANOVA is the natural capstone because it demands every concept introduced earlier—normal distributions, variance estimation, independence, and the $F$-distribution—simultaneously. It is also a doorway. The same decomposition principle extends to two-way designs that separate treatment and block effects, to repeated-measures layouts that account for individual differences, and to regression, where ANOVA tables reappear as a test of overall model fit. Mastery of the one-way case is therefore not a terminus but a base camp.

You are now equipped to pursue any of these directions.
