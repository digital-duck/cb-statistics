Mean Square

Definition. Let $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ be a finite sequence of real numbers. The mean square (MS) of the sequence is defined as

$$\mathrm{MS}(x) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2.$$

More generally, for a continuous random variable $X$ with probability density function $f$, the mean square is the second moment:

$$\mathrm{MS}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx.$$

The mean square must not be confused with the variance $\mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[X^2] - (\mathbb{E}[X])^2$; the two coincide only when $\mathbb{E}[X] = 0$. The positive square root of the mean square is the root mean square (RMS), a quantity of central importance in signal processing and electrical engineering.

Worked Example. Consider the discrete signal $x = (1, -3, 2, 4, -2)$.

$$\mathrm{MS}(x) = \frac{1}{5}\bigl(1^2 + (-3)^2 + 2^2 + 4^2 + (-2)^2\bigr) = \frac{1+9+4+16+4}{5} = \frac{34}{5} = 6.8.$$

The RMS is therefore $\sqrt{6.8} \approx 2.608$. Notice that the sample mean is $\bar{x} = \tfrac{2}{5} = 0.4$, so the variance is $6.8 - 0.16 = 6.64$, confirming the identity $\mathrm{MS} = \mathrm{Var} + \bar{x}^2$.

Key Theorem (Parseval's Identity). For a square-integrable function $f$ on $[-L, L]$ with Fourier coefficients $\{a_k, b_k\}$,

$$\frac{1}{2L}\int_{-L}^{L} [f(x)]^2\,dx = \frac{a_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_k^2 + b_k^2).$$

This states that the mean square of $f$ equals the sum of squared Fourier amplitudes — power is conserved across the frequency domain. Parseval's identity underpins spectral analysis: measuring mean square in the time domain is equivalent to summing power across all harmonics.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Rational, sqrt, symbols, integrate, exp, oo, pi

# Discrete mean square
data = [1, -3, 2, 4, -2]
ms_discrete = Rational(sum(v**2 for v in data), len(data))
rms_discrete = sqrt(ms_discrete)
print("MS =", ms_discrete, "  RMS =", rms_discrete)

# Continuous: MS of X ~ Normal(0,1)
x = symbols('x')
f = exp(-x**2 / 2) / sqrt(2 * pi)
ms_normal = integrate(x**2 * f, (x, -oo, oo))
print("E[X^2] for N(0,1) =", ms_normal)  # Expected: 1

Running this cell confirms $\mathrm{MS} = \tfrac{34}{5}$ for the discrete signal and $\mathbb{E}[X^2] = 1$ for the standard normal, where the mean is zero and variance equals the mean square.
