Concept Book: Test Of Two Variances

Introductory Statistics Ch13 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the test achieves. Throughout this book we have built, layer by layer, the language of variability: sample variance as an unbiased estimator, the chi-squared distribution as the sampling distribution of a single scaled variance, and the ratio of two independent chi-squared variables as the $F$-distribution. The test of two variances—formally, the $F$-test for equality of population variances—is the natural capstone because it is the first inferential tool that simultaneously involves two independent sources of random variation. Given independent samples of sizes $n_1$ and $n_2$ drawn from normal populations $\mathcal{N}(\mu_1,\sigma_1^2)$ and $\mathcal{N}(\mu_2,\sigma_2^2)$, the null hypothesis $H_0\colon \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$ is assessed through the test statistic

$$F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2},$$

which under $H_0$ follows an $F$-distribution with $\nu_1 = n_1-1$ and $\nu_2 = n_2-1$ degrees of freedom. A result far from $1$ in either tail constitutes evidence that the two populations differ in spread, not merely in location.

Worked example. A precision-instrument manufacturer runs two production lines. Line A produces $n_1 = 16$ calibration weights with sample variance $S_1^2 = 0.0144\,\text{g}^2$; Line B produces $n_2 = 21$ weights with $S_2^2 = 0.0049\,\text{g}^2$. The observed statistic is $F_{\text{obs}} = 0.0144/0.0049 \approx 2.94$. At the $\alpha = 0.05$ level with $\nu_1=15$, $\nu_2=20$, the upper critical value is $F_{0.025,15,20} \approx 2.57$. Because $2.94 > 2.57$ we reject $H_0$ and conclude that Line A exhibits significantly greater variability—a finding that triggers a process audit, not merely a mean adjustment.

Key theorem. If $S_1^2$ and $S_2^2$ are the sample variances of independent normal samples, then

$$\frac{S_1^2/\sigma_1^2}{S_2^2/\sigma_2^2} \sim F(\nu_1,\nu_2).$$

Under $H_0$ the unknown ratio $\sigma_1^2/\sigma_2^2$ cancels to $1$, making $F = S_1^2/S_2^2$ a pivot—a statistic whose distribution is fully known and parameter-free. This pivoting argument is the same structural move that underlies the $t$-test; mastering it here equips you to derive analogous tests in regression and ANOVA.

Lab cell.

from scipy import stats

s1_sq, n1 = 0.0144, 16
s2_sq, n2 = 0.0049, 21
F_obs = s1_sq / s2_sq
df1, df2 = n1 - 1, n2 - 1

p_value = 2 * min(
    stats.f.cdf(F_obs, df1, df2),
    stats.f.sf(F_obs, df1, df2)
)
print(f"F = {F_obs:.4f},  p = {p_value:.4f}")
# F = 2.9388,  p = 0.0327

The test of two variances is therefore not merely an isolated procedure—it is a template: form a ratio, identify its null distribution, locate the observed value in the tails. Every major test in the chapters ahead—comparing treatment groups in a clinical trial, assessing model fit in regression, benchmarking algorithmic run-time consistency—inherits this architecture. Pick any one of those arenas and carry the $F$-ratio forward; the mathematics you have learned here will meet you on the other side.
