Sum Of Squares

Definition. Given a finite sequence of real numbers $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, the sum of squares is the quantity

$$SS = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \cdots + x_n^2.$$

More generally, if the sequence represents deviations from a mean $\bar{x}$, we define the corrected sum of squares (also called the total sum of squares in statistics):

$$SS_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2.$$

This quantity measures the total variability in a dataset and forms the foundation of variance, standard deviation, and least-squares regression.

Worked Example. Let $x_1 = 2,\, x_2 = 4,\, x_3 = 6,\, x_4 = 8$. The sample mean is

$$\bar{x} = \frac{2+4+6+8}{4} = 5.$$

The corrected sum of squares is

$$SS_{\text{tot}} = (2-5)^2 + (4-5)^2 + (6-5)^2 + (8-5)^2 = 9 + 1 + 1 + 9 = 20.$$

The sample variance is then $s^2 = SS_{\text{tot}}/(n-1) = 20/3 \approx 6.67$, and the sample standard deviation $s \approx 2.58$.

Key Theorem (Parallel-Axis / Huygens–König Decomposition). For any sequence $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ with mean $\bar{x}$,

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \bar{x})^2 + n\bar{x}^2.$$

Proof sketch. Expand $(x_i - \bar{x})^2 = x_i^2 - 2x_i\bar{x} + \bar{x}^2$ and sum over $i$. The cross-term satisfies $\sum_{i}2x_i\bar{x} = 2\bar{x}\sum_i x_i = 2n\bar{x}^2$, so the right-hand side collapses to $\sum x_i^2 - n\bar{x}^2 + n\bar{x}^2 = \sum x_i^2$. $\blacksquare$

This identity underlies the ANOVA decomposition $SS_{\text{tot}} = SS_{\text{model}} + SS_{\text{residual}}$, a cornerstone of statistical inference.

Lab Cell.

import sympy as sp

data = [2, 4, 6, 8]
n = len(data)
mean = sp.Rational(sum(data), n)          # exact rational arithmetic
ss_tot = sum((sp.Integer(x) - mean)**2 for x in data)
variance = ss_tot / (n - 1)

print("Mean       :", mean)
print("SS_tot     :", ss_tot)
print("Variance   :", variance)
print("Std dev    :", sp.sqrt(variance))

Running this cell confirms $SS_{\text{tot}} = 20$, $s^2 = 20/3$, and $s = 2\sqrt{5}/\sqrt{3}$ exactly, illustrating how symbolic computation avoids floating-point rounding errors present in numerical libraries.
