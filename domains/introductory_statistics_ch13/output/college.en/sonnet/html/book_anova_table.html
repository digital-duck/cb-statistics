Concept Book: Anova Table

Introductory Statistics Ch13 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

An ANOVA table (Analysis of Variance table) is a structured decomposition of the total variability in a response variable into attributable sources, together with the statistical machinery needed to decide whether each source explains more variation than chance alone would predict. Having built up the prerequisite ideas — sum of squares, degrees of freedom, mean squares, and the $F$-distribution — the ANOVA table is the natural endpoint because it consolidates every piece of that machinery into a single, readable object from which decisions flow directly.

Formally, given a balanced one-way design with $k$ groups each of size $n$, the table partitions the total corrected sum of squares

$$SS_{\text{Total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_{ij} - \bar{y})^{2}$$

into a between-groups component $SS_{\text{Between}}$ and a within-groups component $SS_{\text{Within}}$, with corresponding degrees of freedom $k-1$ and $k(n-1)$. The mean squares $MS_{\text{Between}} = SS_{\text{Between}}/(k-1)$ and $MS_{\text{Within}} = SS_{\text{Within}}/[k(n-1)]$ are unbiased estimators of $\sigma^{2}$ under $H_0:\mu_1=\cdots=\mu_k$, yielding the test statistic

$$F = \frac{MS_{\text{Between}}}{MS_{\text{Within}}} \sim F\!\left(k-1,\, k(n-1)\right).$$

Worked example. Suppose three teaching methods are each applied to five students; exam scores (out of 100) are recorded. Computing $SS_{\text{Between}} = 320$, $SS_{\text{Within}} = 480$ gives $MS_{\text{Between}} = 160$, $MS_{\text{Within}} = 32$, and $F = 5.00$ on $(2, 12)$ degrees of freedom. Since $F_{0.05}(2,12) \approx 3.89$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude the methods differ in effectiveness.

Key theorem (Cochran's theorem). Under the normal linear model, $SS_{\text{Between}}/\sigma^{2}$ and $SS_{\text{Within}}/\sigma^{2}$ are independent chi-squared variates with $k-1$ and $k(n-1)$ degrees of freedom, respectively. This independence is precisely what makes the ratio $F$ follow an $F$-distribution and renders the test exact, not merely approximate.

import sympy as sp
from sympy.stats import F as F_dist, P

# Symbolic F-test: P(F > 5) under F(2,12)
k, n = 3, 5
df1, df2 = k - 1, k * (n - 1)
X = F_dist("X", df1, df2)
p_value = P(X > 5)
print(sp.simplify(p_value))   # exact symbolic p-value

The ANOVA table does not merely end the calculation — it opens every domain in which controlled comparison matters. In clinical trials it separates treatment effects from patient-to-patient noise. In engineering it isolates the factor levels that drive yield. In education research it surfaces which instructional designs produce measurable gains. Each application is, at its core, the same question asked of different data: is the signal larger than the background hum of unexplained variation?

Choose any one of these domains and ask: what comes after the $F$-test? The answer — post-hoc comparisons, effect sizes, mixed models — is a rich landscape that begins exactly where this table leaves off.
