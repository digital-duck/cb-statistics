F Ratio

Definition. The F ratio (or F statistic) is the quotient of two independent mean-square estimates of a common population variance $\sigma^2$:

$$F = \frac{MS_{\text{between}}}{MS_{\text{within}}},$$

where each mean square is a sum of squares divided by its degrees of freedom—a quantity introduced in the preceding section. In a one-way analysis of variance with $k$ groups each of size $n$, the numerator carries $df_1 = k - 1$ degrees of freedom and the denominator carries $df_2 = k(n-1)$ degrees of freedom. When all group population means are equal, both mean squares estimate the same $\sigma^2$ and $F \approx 1$; systematic differences among group means inflate $MS_{\text{between}}$ alone, pushing $F$ above 1.

Worked Example. Suppose three fertiliser formulas are each applied to four plots, yielding group means $\bar{y}_{1\cdot} = 12$, $\bar{y}_{2\cdot} = 15$, $\bar{y}_{3\cdot} = 13$ with grand mean $\bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot} = 13.33$ and pooled within-group mean square $MS_{\text{within}} = 4.0$. The between-group sum of squares is

$$SS_{\text{between}} = n\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\bar{y}_{i\cdot} - \bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2 = 4\bigl[(12-13.33)^2 + (15-13.33)^2 + (13-13.33)^2\bigr] \approx 18.67.$$

With $df_1 = 2$, we obtain $MS_{\text{between}} = 18.67/2 = 9.33$, so $F = 9.33/4.0 = 2.33$. Comparing this observed value to the $F(2,9)$ distribution yields the $p$-value.

Key Theorem (Cochran, 1934). Under the null hypothesis $H_0\colon \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$ with all observations drawn independently from $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the F ratio follows an $F$-distribution with parameters $df_1 = k-1$ and $df_2 = N-k$, where $N = kn$:

$$F \sim F(k-1,\; N-k).$$

The $F$-distribution has support on $(0, \infty)$; its right tail provides the rejection region for the one-sided test of any between-group variation.

Lab Cell.

from sympy.stats import F as F_dist, cdf
from sympy import symbols

df1, df2 = 2, 9
F_rv = F_dist('F', df1, df2)

x = symbols('x', positive=True)
f_obs = 2.33

# P-value: P(F > f_obs)
p_value = 1 - float(cdf(F_rv)(f_obs).doit().evalf())
print(f"F({df1}, {df2}) = {f_obs}, p-value ≈ {p_value:.4f}")
