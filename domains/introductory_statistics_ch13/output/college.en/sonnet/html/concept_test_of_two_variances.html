Test Of Two Variances

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_m \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)$ and $Y_1, Y_2, \ldots, Y_n \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)$ be independent random samples. The test of two variances (also called the $F$-test for equality of variances) assesses the null hypothesis

$$H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2 \qquad \text{against} \qquad H_a: \sigma_1^2 \neq \sigma_2^2$$

using the test statistic

$$F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2},$$

where $S_1^2$ and $S_2^2$ are the sample variances. Under $H_0$, this statistic follows an $F$-distribution with degrees of freedom $\nu_1 = m - 1$ and $\nu_2 = n - 1$, written $F \sim F(\nu_1, \nu_2)$. The $F$-distribution is right-skewed and strictly positive; its mean is $\nu_2/(\nu_2 - 2)$ for $\nu_2 > 2$.

Worked Example. A quality engineer measures the diameter (mm) of bolts from two suppliers. Supplier A yields $m = 16$ bolts with $S_1^2 = 0.036$; Supplier B yields $n = 21$ bolts with $S_2^2 = 0.012$. Compute

$$F_{\text{obs}} = \frac{0.036}{0.012} = 3.00, \qquad \nu_1 = 15, \quad \nu_2 = 20.$$

For a two-sided test at significance level $\alpha = 0.05$, the critical values are $F_{0.025}(15, 20) \approx 2.57$ and $F_{0.975}(15, 20) \approx 0.41$. Since $F_{\text{obs}} = 3.00 > 2.57$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude the two suppliers differ significantly in variability.

Key Theorem. The $F$-distribution arises directly from the chi-squared distributions encountered in the previous section. If $U \sim \chi^2(\nu_1)$ and $V \sim \chi^2(\nu_2)$ are independent, then

$$F = \frac{U/\nu_1}{V/\nu_2} \sim F(\nu_1, \nu_2).$$

Because $(m-1)S_1^2/\sigma_1^2 \sim \chi^2(m-1)$, the ratio $S_1^2/S_2^2$ under $H_0$ is precisely of this form, establishing the null distribution without requiring any additional assumptions beyond normality and independence.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from scipy import stats

S1_sq, S2_sq = 0.036, 0.012
nu1, nu2 = 15, 20
F_obs = S1_sq / S2_sq

p_value = 2 * min(
    stats.f.cdf(F_obs, nu1, nu2),
    stats.f.sf(F_obs, nu1, nu2)
)
print(f"F = {F_obs:.4f},  p-value = {p_value:.4f}")
# F = 3.0000,  p-value = 0.0285

The $p$-value $\approx 0.029 < 0.05$ confirms the rejection of $H_0$, consistent with the critical-value approach above.
