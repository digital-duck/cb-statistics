Concept Book: Test Of Two Variances

Introductory Statistics Ch13 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

The journey through descriptive statistics, probability distributions, and single-sample inference has been building toward a deceptively simple question: are two populations equally variable? The test of two variances — formally, the $F$-test for equality of variances — answers that question rigorously, and in doing so it becomes the gatekeeper to a wide class of downstream analyses.

What it achieves. Given independent random samples of sizes $n_1$ and $n_2$ drawn from normal populations with unknown variances $\sigma_1^2$ and $\sigma_2^2$, the null hypothesis $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$ is tested via the statistic

$$F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2},$$

where $S_i^2$ is the sample variance of group $i$. Under $H_0$ this ratio follows an $F$-distribution with degrees of freedom $\nu_1 = n_1 - 1$ and $\nu_2 = n_2 - 1$:

$$F \sim F(\nu_1,\, \nu_2).$$

A value far from 1 in either tail — specifically, $F < F_{\alpha/2,\nu_1,\nu_2}$ or $F > F_{1-\alpha/2,\nu_1,\nu_2}$ — constitutes evidence against $H_0$ at significance level $\alpha$.

Worked example. A manufacturer measures the fill weight (grams) of a bottling line before and after a mechanical adjustment. Twelve bottles from the old setting give $S_1^2 = 0.84$; ten bottles from the new setting give $S_2^2 = 0.31$. The test statistic is $F = 0.84/0.31 \approx 2.71$ on $\nu_1 = 11$, $\nu_2 = 9$ degrees of freedom. The upper critical value at $\alpha = 0.05$ is $F_{0.975,11,9} \approx 3.96$. Because $2.71 < 3.96$, we fail to reject $H_0$: the adjustment has not demonstrably changed process variability.

Key theorem (natural endpoint). The $F$-test is the likelihood-ratio test for $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$ under bivariate normality, making it the uniformly most powerful unbiased test in that setting (Lehmann & Romano, Testing Statistical Hypotheses, §5.4). This optimality property explains why it appears as a prerequisite step inside two-sample $t$-tests, ANOVA, and regression diagnostics — those procedures assume equal variances, and the $F$-test is precisely the tool that validates or refutes that assumption before the analysis proceeds.

Lab cell.

from scipy import stats

s1_sq, n1 = 0.84, 12
s2_sq, n2 = 0.31, 10
F_stat = s1_sq / s2_sq
df1, df2 = n1 - 1, n2 - 1

p_value = 2 * min(
    stats.f.cdf(F_stat, df1, df2),
    stats.f.sf(F_stat, df1, df2)
)
print(f"F = {F_stat:.4f},  p = {p_value:.4f}")

With the $F$-test in hand, you are no longer confined to asking whether two means differ — you can ask whether two processes, two instruments, or two experimental conditions are equally precise. That question lies at the heart of quality engineering, clinical bioequivalence assessment, and financial risk comparison alike. Choose one of those domains and carry this single statistic as far as it will take you: the results may surprise you.
