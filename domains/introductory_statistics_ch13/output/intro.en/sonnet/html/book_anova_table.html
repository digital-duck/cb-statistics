What the ANOVA Table Achieves

Throughout this text, we have built a precise vocabulary for decomposing variation: sums of squares, degrees of freedom, mean squares, and the $F$-ratio. The ANOVA table is the instrument that assembles every one of those quantities into a single, auditable record of a statistical decision. Rather than reporting a bare $p$-value, the table forces the analyst to account for where variance lives — between treatment groups or within them — and to confirm that the ratio of those two estimates is large enough to be implausible under the null hypothesis $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$.

Formally, for a one-way design with $k$ groups and $N$ total observations, the table encodes the partition

$$SS_{\text{Total}} = SS_{\text{Between}} + SS_{\text{Within}},$$

alongside the corresponding degrees of freedom $df_B = k - 1$ and $df_W = N - k$, the mean squares $MS_B = SS_B / df_B$ and $MS_W = SS_W / df_W$, and the test statistic

$$F = \frac{MS_B}{MS_W} \sim F(k-1,\, N-k) \quad \text{under } H_0.$$

Every cell of the table is a check: the degrees of freedom must sum to $N-1$; the mean squares must be positive; the $F$-value must equal the ratio of the preceding column's entries. A table that passes these checks is self-certifying.

Worked Example

Suppose a nutritionist measures seedling heights (cm) under three fertiliser treatments ($k = 3$), with five replicates each ($N = 15$). After computing group means $\bar{y}_{1\cdot}, \bar{y}_{2\cdot}, \bar{y}_{3\cdot}$ and the grand mean $\bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot}$, she obtains $SS_B = 48.4$ and $SS_W = 30.6$.

| Source | $SS$ | $df$ | $MS$ | $F$ | |---|---|---|---|---| | Between | 48.4 | 2 | 24.20 | 9.48 | | Within | 30.6 | 12 | 2.55 | — | | Total | 79.0 | 14 | — | — |

The critical value $F_{0.05}(2, 12) \approx 3.89$. Because $9.48 > 3.89$, she rejects $H_0$ and concludes that at least one fertiliser mean differs from the others.

Key Theorem

Cochran's Theorem. If $y_{ij} \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma^2)$, then $SS_B / \sigma^2$ and $SS_W / \sigma^2$ are independent chi-squared random variables with $k-1$ and $N-k$ degrees of freedom, respectively. Their ratio therefore follows an exact $F$-distribution under $H_0$, guaranteeing that the $p$-value reported in the ANOVA table has its nominal Type I error rate.

This independence — the heart of why the $F$-test is exact rather than approximate — is a direct consequence of the orthogonal decomposition of the observation space into treatment and residual subspaces.

Lab Cell

import sympy as sp
from sympy.stats import F as F_dist, cdf

# Symbolic F critical value at alpha = 0.05, df1=2, df2=12
alpha = sp.Rational(1, 20)
F_rv = F_dist('F', 2, 12)
# Evaluate P(F > 9.48) numerically
p_val = 1 - float(cdf(F_rv)(sp.Float('9.48')))
print(f"p-value ≈ {p_val:.4f}")   # expected: ≈ 0.0034

Running this cell confirms that the obtained $F$-ratio falls deep into the rejection region — a result that would be invisible from the raw data alone, but is immediately legible from the table.

Invitation

The ANOVA table is not an endpoint but a gateway. Once you can read and construct it fluently, you can extend it in any direction the problem demands: add a second crossed factor and the table grows by one row to become a two-way design; replace the $F$-test with a permutation distribution and the normality assumption dissolves; embed the table inside a mixed-effects model and the within-group term splits into fixed and random components. Choose the application domain that matches your own data — agricultural trials, clinical experiments, educational assessments, or spectroscopic measurements — and follow one of those paths to its conclusion. The table will be waiting at every step, organizing the evidence and keeping the accounting honest.
