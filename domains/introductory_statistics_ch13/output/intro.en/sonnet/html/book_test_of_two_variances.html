Concept Book: Test Of Two Variances

Introductory Statistics Ch13 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

What the test achieves. Every two-sample comparison in classical statistics rests, explicitly or silently, on an assumption about the spread of the two populations being compared. The test of two variances — formally the $F$-test for equality of population variances — is the instrument that makes that assumption auditable. Given independent random samples of sizes $n_1$ and $n_2$ drawn from normal populations with unknown variances $\sigma_1^2$ and $\sigma_2^2$, the null hypothesis $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$ is evaluated through the test statistic

$$F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2},$$

where $S_i^2$ denotes the sample variance of group $i$. Under $H_0$, this ratio follows the $F$-distribution with degrees of freedom $\nu_1 = n_1 - 1$ and $\nu_2 = n_2 - 1$. A value far from $1$ in either tail furnishes evidence against equality of spread.

Why it is the natural endpoint. The book has progressed through single-population inference (means, then variances) and then crossed into two-population territory for means. Completing that matrix requires confronting variances in the two-population case. The $F$-test closes that logical square: it is the last cell in a $2 \times 2$ table of first-order inferential problems, and its sampling distribution, the $F$-distribution, unifies the chi-squared results of earlier chapters because $F(\nu_1, \nu_2)$ is the ratio of two independent $\chi^2$ variables each divided by its degrees of freedom.

A concrete illustration. A quality engineer measures the tensile strength (in MPa) of steel rods from two suppliers, obtaining $S_1^2 = 4.2$ from $n_1 = 16$ rods and $S_2^2 = 1.8$ from $n_2 = 21$ rods. The observed statistic is

$$F_{\text{obs}} = \frac{4.2}{1.8} \approx 2.33,$$

compared against $F_{0.025}(15, 20) \approx 2.57$ at the $5\%$ two-tailed level. Because $2.33 < 2.57$, the engineer fails to reject $H_0$ — a consequential finding that licenses the pooled-variance $t$-test for comparing the two suppliers' mean strengths.

Key theorem. If $S_1^2$ and $S_2^2$ are the sample variances of independent normal samples, then under $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$,

$$F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2} \sim F(\nu_1,\, \nu_2).$$

The $p$-value is $p = 2\min\!\bigl[P(F \leq F_{\text{obs}}),\, P(F \geq F_{\text{obs}})\bigr]$ for a two-sided alternative.

Lab cell.

from scipy import stats

s1_sq, n1 = 4.2, 16
s2_sq, n2 = 1.8, 21
F_obs = s1_sq / s2_sq
nu1, nu2 = n1 - 1, n2 - 1

p_value = 2 * min(stats.f.cdf(F_obs, nu1, nu2),
                  stats.f.sf(F_obs, nu1, nu2))
print(f"F = {F_obs:.4f}, p = {p_value:.4f}")

The road does not end here. Every domain that compares two processes — manufacturing tolerances, clinical-trial outcome variability, financial return volatility — ultimately asks whether two spreads differ. Choose one of these domains that speaks to you and carry the $F$-test into it; the machinery is now entirely in your hands.
