What One-Way ANOVA Achieves

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) is the principled answer to a question that arises constantly in empirical science: do the means of three or more groups differ beyond what random chance alone would produce? Where a two-sample $t$-test compares a single pair of group means, one-way ANOVA compares $k \geq 2$ groups simultaneously while holding the family-wise Type I error rate at the chosen $\alpha$. It does so by decomposing the total variability in observed data into two orthogonal sources—variance between groups and variance within groups—and forming a ratio that follows an $F$-distribution under the null hypothesis of equal population means.

Formally, given $k$ independent groups with $n_i$ observations each, the model is

$$X_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \varepsilon_{ij}, \qquad \varepsilon_{ij} \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),$$

where $\mu$ is the grand mean and $\tau_i$ is the treatment effect for group $i$, subject to $\sum_i n_i \tau_i = 0$. The null hypothesis $H_0\colon \tau_1 = \tau_2 = \cdots = \tau_k = 0$ is tested via the $F$-statistic

$$F = \frac{\mathrm{MS}_{\text{between}}}{\mathrm{MS}_{\text{within}}} = \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\text{between}}/(k-1)}{\mathrm{SS}_{\text{within}}/(N-k)},$$

which under $H_0$ follows an $F(k-1,\, N-k)$ distribution.

Worked Example

Suppose a plant biologist measures the stem height (cm) of seedlings grown under three light intensities: low, medium, and high, with $n = 5$ replicates each. Sample means are $\bar{x}_1 = 12.4$, $\bar{x}_2 = 15.1$, $\bar{x}_3 = 18.7$ and the grand mean is $\bar{x} = 15.4$. Computing $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{between}} = 5[(12.4-15.4)^2+(15.1-15.4)^2+(18.7-15.4)^2] = 5[9+0.09+10.89] = 99.9$ and $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{within}} = 22.4$ (pooled within-group deviations) yields $F = (99.9/2)/(22.4/12) \approx 26.8$. Since $F_{0.05}(2,12) \approx 3.89$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude that light intensity significantly affects growth.

Key Theorem

Cochran's Theorem guarantees that $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{between}}/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2(k-1)$ and $\mathrm{SS}_{\text{within}}/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2(N-k)$ are independent under the normality assumption, establishing the exact $F$-null distribution and making inference valid in finite samples.

Lab Cell

from scipy import stats
low    = [11.2, 12.8, 13.1, 11.9, 13.0]
medium = [14.5, 15.3, 16.0, 14.9, 14.8]
high   = [18.1, 19.2, 18.5, 18.9, 19.0]
F, p = stats.f_oneway(low, medium, high)
print(f"F = {F:.3f},  p = {p:.4f}")

Invitation

One-way ANOVA is the gateway to a rich family of designs—blocked experiments, factorial arrangements, repeated measures, and mixed models—each of which relaxes or extends one of the assumptions explored here. To see how these ideas operate in a domain you care about, choose any of the application areas introduced in this text and carry a real dataset all the way from exploratory plots through post-hoc pairwise comparisons.
