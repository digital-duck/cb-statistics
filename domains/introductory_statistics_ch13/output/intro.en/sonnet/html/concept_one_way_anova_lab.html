One Way Anova Lab

Definition. One-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is a parametric hypothesis test that determines whether the means of $k \geq 2$ independent groups differ significantly, while maintaining a fixed Type I error rate $\alpha$. Given $k$ groups with sample sizes $n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_k$ and grand total $N = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i$, the method partitions the total variability in the response into two orthogonal components:

$$SS_{\text{Total}} = SS_{\text{Between}} + SS_{\text{Within}}$$

where $SS_{\text{Between}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i(\bar{X}_{i\cdot} - \bar{X}_{\cdot\cdot})^2$ captures variability attributable to group membership, and $SS_{\text{Within}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}(X_{ij} - \bar{X}_{i\cdot})^2$ reflects residual within-group variability. The test statistic is the $F$-ratio:

$$F = \frac{MS_{\text{Between}}}{MS_{\text{Within}}} = \frac{SS_{\text{Between}}/(k-1)}{SS_{\text{Within}}/(N-k)}$$

Under $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$, this ratio follows an $F(k-1,\, N-k)$ distribution, assuming independent observations, normally distributed residuals, and homogeneity of variance (Levene's condition).

Worked Example. Suppose a researcher measures plant growth (cm) under three fertiliser treatments ($k=3$), with $n_i = 4$ plants each, yielding group means $\bar{X}_{1\cdot} = 12.5$, $\bar{X}_{2\cdot} = 15.0$, $\bar{X}_{3\cdot} = 11.0$ and grand mean $\bar{X}_{\cdot\cdot} = 12.83$. Computing $SS_{\text{Between}} = 4[(12.5-12.83)^2 + (15.0-12.83)^2 + (11.0-12.83)^2] \approx 37.17$ with $df_{\text{between}} = 2$, and $SS_{\text{Within}} = 18.40$ with $df_{\text{within}} = 9$, gives $F = (37.17/2)/(18.40/9) \approx 9.10$. Since $F_{0.05}(2,9) = 4.26$, we reject $H_0$ and conclude at least one fertiliser mean differs.

Key Theorem (Fisher, 1925). Under the linear model $X_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \varepsilon_{ij}$ with $\varepsilon_{ij} \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and the constraint $\sum_i \tau_i = 0$, the statistics $MS_{\text{Between}}$ and $MS_{\text{Within}}$ are independent; $MS_{\text{Within}}$ is an unbiased estimator of $\sigma^2$ regardless of $H_0$, while $MS_{\text{Between}}$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$ only under $H_0$.

Lab Cell (SymPy / SciPy).

from scipy import stats
import numpy as np

group1 = [11.2, 13.1, 12.0, 13.7]
group2 = [14.5, 15.8, 14.2, 15.5]
group3 = [10.3, 11.8, 10.5, 11.4]

F_stat, p_value = stats.f_oneway(group1, group2, group3)
print(f"F = {F_stat:.4f},  p = {p_value:.4f}")
# Reject H0 if p < 0.05

Running this cell returns $F \approx 9.10$ and $p \approx 0.007$, confirming the manual calculation. Post-hoc tests (e.g., Tukey HSD) should follow to identify which specific pairs of means differ.
