Concept Book: Goodness Of Fit Lab

Introductory Statistics Ch11 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

A statistical model earns its keep only when its assumed distribution can be held accountable against observed data. The goodness_of_fit_lab consolidates every tool developed in this textbook — empirical histograms, cumulative distribution functions, moment estimation, and hypothesis testing — into a single reproducible workflow that answers one precise question: is the distribution I have assumed a plausible generator of the data I have collected?

Formally, let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be an i.i.d. sample and let $F_0$ denote a hypothesised cumulative distribution function, possibly depending on parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ estimated from the sample. A goodness-of-fit procedure constructs a discrepancy statistic $T_n = T(X_1,\ldots,X_n; F_0)$ and evaluates the $p$-value $p = \Pr(T_n \geq t_{\text{obs}} \mid H_0)$. Rejection at level $\alpha$ implies that the observed deviation from $F_0$ would be improbable under the null model; failure to reject licenses the practitioner to proceed with $F_0$ as a working assumption.

Worked example. Suppose $n = 200$ inter-arrival times are recorded at a service desk. The sample mean is $\bar{x} = 4.3$ seconds. We hypothesise exponential arrivals: $H_0\colon F_0(x) = 1 - e^{-x/4.3}$. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic is $D_n = \sup_x \lvert \hat{F}_n(x) - F_0(x)\rvert$. For $n=200$ and $D_{200} = 0.041$, the asymptotic $p$-value exceeds $0.40$; we retain the exponential model and proceed to queue-length analysis with confidence.

Key theorem (Glivenko–Cantelli). For any continuous $F$, the empirical CDF $\hat{F}_n$ satisfies $\sup_x \lvert \hat{F}_n(x) - F(x)\rvert \xrightarrow{a.s.} 0$ as $n\to\infty$. This guarantees that, under the true distribution, discrepancy statistics converge to zero, providing the asymptotic foundation on which all goodness-of-fit critical values rest.

from sympy.stats import Normal, cdf, sample
from scipy.stats import kstest
import numpy as np

data = np.random.exponential(scale=4.3, size=200)
stat, p = kstest(data, 'expon', args=(0, data.mean()))
print(f"KS statistic: {stat:.4f},  p-value: {p:.4f}")

The techniques in goodness_of_fit_lab are not endpoints in themselves; they are keys. Every domain that relies on a parametric likelihood — survival analysis in clinical trials, risk modelling in actuarial science, residual diagnostics in linear regression, and distributional assumptions in machine-learning pipelines — depends on exactly the validation machinery assembled here.
