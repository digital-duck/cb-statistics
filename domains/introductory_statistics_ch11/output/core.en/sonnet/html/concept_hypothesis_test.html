Hypothesis Test

Definition. A hypothesis test is a formal procedure for deciding, on the basis of observed data, whether to reject a stated claim about a population parameter. The procedure begins with two competing statements: the null hypothesis $H_0$, which asserts a specific value or range for the parameter, and the alternative hypothesis $H_1$ (or $H_a$), which contradicts $H_0$. A test statistic $T$ is computed from the sample and compared against a reference distribution derived under the assumption that $H_0$ is true. If $T$ falls in the rejection region — the set of values sufficiently improbable under $H_0$ — then $H_0$ is rejected at significance level $\alpha$, where $\alpha = P(\text{reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ true})$ is the pre-specified probability of a Type I error.

Worked Example. A manufacturer claims that the mean fill weight of cereal boxes is $\mu_0 = 500\,\mathrm{g}$. A quality inspector draws a random sample of $n = 36$ boxes and records a sample mean $\bar{x} = 496\,\mathrm{g}$ with known population standard deviation $\sigma = 12\,\mathrm{g}$. Setting $H_0: \mu = 500$ against $H_1: \mu \neq 500$ (two-tailed), the test statistic is

$$Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} = \frac{496 - 500}{12/\sqrt{36}} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2.00.$$

At significance level $\alpha = 0.05$, the critical values for a two-tailed $z$-test are $\pm 1.96$. Because $|{-2.00}| > 1.96$, the inspector rejects $H_0$ and concludes that the fill weight differs significantly from $500\,\mathrm{g}$.

Key Theorem (Neyman–Pearson Lemma). Among all tests of $H_0: \theta = \theta_0$ versus $H_1: \theta = \theta_1$ at level $\alpha$, the likelihood ratio test — which rejects $H_0$ when $\Lambda = \mathcal{L}(\theta_1)/\mathcal{L}(\theta_0) > k$ for an appropriate threshold $k$ — maximises the power $1 - \beta = P(\text{reject } H_0 \mid H_1 \text{ true})$. This establishes the likelihood ratio as the most efficient decision rule for simple hypotheses.

Lab Cell.

from scipy import stats

x_bar, mu0, sigma, n = 496, 500, 12, 36
z_stat = (x_bar - mu0) / (sigma / n**0.5)
p_value = 2 * stats.norm.sf(abs(z_stat))   # two-tailed p-value

print(f"z = {z_stat:.4f}")
print(f"p-value = {p_value:.4f}")
print("Reject H0" if p_value < 0.05 else "Fail to reject H0")

Running this cell yields $z = -2.0000$ and $p = 0.0455 < 0.05$, confirming rejection of $H_0$ and illustrating how the $p$-value quantifies the probability of observing a result at least as extreme as $\bar{x} = 496\,\mathrm{g}$ when $H_0$ holds.
