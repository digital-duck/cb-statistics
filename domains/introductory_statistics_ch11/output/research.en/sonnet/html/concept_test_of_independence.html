Test Of Independence

Definition. Given two categorical random variables $X$ and $Y$ defined on the same probability space, we say $X$ and $Y$ are statistically independent if and only if their joint distribution factors as the product of their marginals:

$$P(X = x_i,\; Y = y_j) = P(X = x_i)\,P(Y = y_j) \quad \text{for all } i, j.$$

The test of independence is a hypothesis test that uses observed frequency data to evaluate whether this factorization holds in a population. The null hypothesis is $H_0$: $X$ and $Y$ are independent; the alternative is $H_1$: they are not.

Worked Example. A survey of 200 students records preferred study environment (Library, Café, Home) and academic year (First, Second). The observed counts are arranged in a $2 \times 3$ contingency table:

| | Library | Café | Home | Row total | |------------|---------|------|------|-----------| | First year | 40 | 30 | 20 | 90 | | Second year| 50 | 40 | 20 | 110 | | Col total | 90 | 70 | 40 | 200 |

Under $H_0$, the expected count for cell $(i,j)$ is:

$$E_{ij} = \frac{(\text{row}_i\text{ total})\times(\text{col}_j\text{ total})}{n}.$$

For example, $E_{11} = (90 \times 90)/200 = 40.5$. The test statistic aggregates deviations across all cells:

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i,j} \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}.$$

Computing each term and summing gives $\chi^2 \approx 0.97$.

Key Theorem (Pearson, 1900). Under $H_0$ and as $n \to \infty$, the statistic $\chi^2$ converges in distribution to a chi-squared distribution with $(r-1)(c-1)$ degrees of freedom, where $r$ and $c$ are the number of rows and columns respectively. For a $2\times 3$ table, $\nu = (2-1)(3-1) = 2$. At significance level $\alpha = 0.05$, the critical value is $\chi^2_{2,\,0.05} \approx 5.99$. Since $0.97 < 5.99$, we fail to reject $H_0$; the data provide no significant evidence of an association between year and study preference.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Rational

O = [[40, 30, 20], [50, 40, 20]]
n = 200
row_totals = [sum(row) for row in O]
col_totals = [sum(O[i][j] for i in range(len(O))) for j in range(len(O[0]))]

chi2_stat = sum(
    (O[i][j] - Rational(row_totals[i] * col_totals[j], n))**2
    / Rational(row_totals[i] * col_totals[j], n)
    for i in range(len(O)) for j in range(len(O[0]))
)
print("chi2 =", float(chi2_stat))   # Expected ≈ 0.97
