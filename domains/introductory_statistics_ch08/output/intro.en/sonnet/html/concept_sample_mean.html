Sample Mean

Definition. Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ be a finite collection of $n$ real-valued observations drawn from a population. The sample mean is the arithmetic average of these observations, defined by

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i.$$

The sample mean $\bar{x}$ is a statistic — a function of the observed data alone — and serves as the canonical point estimator for the unknown population mean $\mu$. Unlike $\mu$, which is a fixed but typically unknowable parameter, $\bar{x}$ varies from sample to sample and is itself a random variable when regarded before data collection.

Worked Example. A laboratory records the resting heart rates (beats per minute) of five volunteers: $62, 75, 81, 68, 74$. Compute the sample mean.

$$\bar{x} = \frac{62 + 75 + 81 + 68 + 74}{5} = \frac{360}{5} = 72.$$

The sample mean is $72$ bpm. Note that no individual observation equals $72$; the sample mean characterises the centre of mass of the dataset, not a value that must appear in it.

Key Theorem (Unbiasedness and Variance of $\bar{X}$). Suppose $X_1, \dots, X_n$ are independent and identically distributed random variables, each with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2 < \infty$. Then

$$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \mu \qquad \text{and} \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}.$$

Proof sketch. By linearity of expectation, $\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \mu$. Independence yields $\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(X_i) = \sigma^2/n$. $\square$

The factor $1/n$ in the variance is fundamental: collecting more data shrinks the spread of $\bar{X}$ around $\mu$, which is the quantitative content of the Law of Large Numbers.

from sympy import symbols, Rational, summation, simplify

# Symbolic sample mean for n observations
n, i = symbols('n i', positive=True, integer=True)
x = symbols('x')

# Concrete verification
data = [62, 75, 81, 68, 74]
xbar = Rational(sum(data), len(data))
print("Sample mean:", xbar)          # 72
print("Unbiasedness holds by linearity of E[·]")
