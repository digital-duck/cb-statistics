Concept Book: Confidence Interval Application

Introductory Statistics Ch08 · Generated by SPL

Payoff

Definition. A confidence interval application is the deployment of an interval estimator $[\hat{\theta}_L, \hat{\theta}_U]$ — constructed from sample data — to answer a concrete inferential question about an unknown population parameter $\theta$. Formally, given a random sample $X_1, \ldots, X_n \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} F_\theta$, a $(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval satisfies

$$P\!\left(\hat{\theta}_L \leq \theta \leq \hat{\theta}_U\right) = 1 - \alpha,$$

where the probability is taken over repeated sampling. The interval does not assert that $\theta$ is random; rather, it quantifies the long-run reliability of the estimation procedure.

This concept is the natural endpoint of the journey through sampling distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, and point estimation. Each earlier idea was scaffolding: the CLT guaranteed that $\bar{X}$ is approximately normal; the standard error measured its spread; and now the confidence interval transforms that spread into a decision-quality guarantee.

Worked example. A quality-control engineer measures the tensile strength (MPa) of $n = 40$ steel rods, obtaining $\bar{x} = 412.3$ and sample standard deviation $s = 18.6$. Invoking the CLT, the 95% confidence interval for the true mean $\mu$ is

$$\bar{x} \;\pm\; z_{0.025}\,\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} \;=\; 412.3 \;\pm\; 1.96 \cdot \frac{18.6}{\sqrt{40}} \;\approx\; [406.5,\;418.1]\;\text{MPa}.$$

The engineer can report, with 95% confidence, that the production process meets the 400 MPa specification minimum.

Key theorem (Asymptotic coverage). Let $\hat{\theta}$ be an asymptotically normal estimator with standard error $\text{se}(\hat{\theta})$. Then the interval $\hat{\theta} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\text{se}(\hat{\theta})$ achieves nominal coverage $1-\alpha$ as $n \to \infty$, regardless of the underlying distribution $F_\theta$, provided $F_\theta$ has finite variance.

Lab cell.

import sympy as sp

x_bar, s, n, z = 412.3, 18.6, 40, 1.96
se = s / sp.sqrt(n)
lower = x_bar - z * se
upper = x_bar + z * se
sp.pprint(sp.Interval(sp.N(lower, 5), sp.N(upper, 5)))

Confidence interval application is the point at which statistical theory becomes an instrument of action — in medicine, engineering, economics, and public policy alike. To see its full power, turn to the next chapter, where we construct survival-adjusted confidence intervals for clinical trial endpoints and discover how the width of an interval can literally determine whether a drug reaches patients.
