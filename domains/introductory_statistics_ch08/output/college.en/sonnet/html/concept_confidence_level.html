Confidence Level

Definition. Let $\theta$ be an unknown population parameter and let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be a random sample from the population. A confidence interval for $\theta$ at confidence level $1 - \alpha$ is a random interval $[L(\mathbf{X}),\, U(\mathbf{X})]$, constructed from the sample, such that

$$P\!\bigl(L(\mathbf{X}) \le \theta \le U(\mathbf{X})\bigr) = 1 - \alpha,$$

where $0 < \alpha < 1$. The quantity $1 - \alpha$ is called the confidence level (or coverage probability) of the interval, and $\alpha$ is the significance level. Common choices are $1 - \alpha \in \{0.90,\, 0.95,\, 0.99\}$. Critically, the probability statement refers to the procedure: across many independent replications, $(1-\alpha)\times 100\%$ of the resulting intervals will contain $\theta$. For any single realized interval, $\theta$ either lies inside it or does not.

Worked Example. Suppose $X_1,\ldots,X_{25}$ are drawn from $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma = 4$ known, and the observed sample mean is $\bar{x} = 31.2$. We seek a 95% confidence interval for $\mu$ (so $\alpha = 0.05$). Because $\bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\, \sigma^2/n)$, the pivot

$$Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

satisfies $P(-z_{\alpha/2} \le Z \le z_{\alpha/2}) = 0.95$, where $z_{0.025} = 1.96$. Rearranging gives

$$\bar{X} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = 31.2 \pm 1.96 \cdot \frac{4}{\sqrt{25}} = 31.2 \pm 1.568.$$

The 95% confidence interval is $[29.632,\; 32.768]$.

Theorem (Coverage–Width Trade-off). For a fixed sample size $n$ and fixed $\sigma$, the width of a normal confidence interval,

$$W = 2\, z_{\alpha/2}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}},$$

is strictly increasing in $1 - \alpha$. Consequently, increasing confidence level necessarily widens the interval; the only way to achieve higher confidence without sacrificing precision is to increase $n$.

Proof sketch. Since $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the upper $\alpha/2$ quantile of the standard normal and is strictly decreasing in $\alpha$, it follows that $W$ increases as $\alpha \to 0$ (i.e., as $1-\alpha \to 1$). $\square$

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import sqrt, N

alpha, z_half = 0.05, 1.96          # 95% level
xbar, sigma, n = 31.2, 4, 25
margin = z_half * sigma / sqrt(n)
lower, upper = xbar - margin, xbar + margin
print(f"95% CI: [{N(lower, 5)}, {N(upper, 5)}]")

# Explore trade-off: width vs. confidence level
for conf, z in [(0.90, 1.645), (0.95, 1.960), (0.99, 2.576)]:
    w = 2 * z * sigma / sqrt(n)
    print(f"Confidence {conf:.0%}: width = {N(w, 4)}")
