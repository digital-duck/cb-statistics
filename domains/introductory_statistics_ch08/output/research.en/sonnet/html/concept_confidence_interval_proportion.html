Confidence Interval Proportion

Definition. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be independent Bernoulli random variables with unknown success probability $p \in (0,1)$. The sample proportion is the statistic

$$\hat{p} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i.$$

A confidence interval for a proportion is an interval $(\hat{p}_L, \hat{p}_U)$, constructed from the data, such that

$$P(\hat{p}_L \leq p \leq \hat{p}_U) = 1 - \alpha,$$

where $1 - \alpha$ is the confidence level (commonly $0.95$). The interval does not assert that $p$ is random; rather, the procedure produces intervals that contain the true $p$ in $(1-\alpha) \times 100\%$ of repeated experiments.

By the Central Limit Theorem, for large $n$ the sampling distribution of $\hat{p}$ is approximately normal:

$$\hat{p} \;\approx\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(p,\; \frac{p(1-p)}{n}\right).$$

Replacing the unknown $p$ in the variance with $\hat{p}$ yields the Wald interval:

$$\hat{p} \;\pm\; z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}},$$

where $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the upper $\alpha/2$ quantile of the standard normal (e.g., $z_{0.025} \approx 1.96$ for a $95\%$ interval). The quantity $z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n}$ is called the margin of error.

Worked Example. A survey of $n = 400$ voters finds $\hat{p} = 0.58$ supporting a ballot measure. Construct a $95\%$ confidence interval.

$$\text{SE} = \sqrt{\frac{0.58 \times 0.42}{400}} = \sqrt{0.0006090} \approx 0.02468.$$ $$\text{CI} = 0.58 \pm 1.96 \times 0.02468 = 0.58 \pm 0.0484 = (0.5316,\; 0.6284).$$

We conclude with $95\%$ confidence that the true proportion of supporters lies between $53.2\%$ and $62.8\%$.

Theorem (Coverage Validity). The Wald interval achieves nominal coverage $1-\alpha$ asymptotically as $n \to \infty$, provided $n\hat{p} \geq 5$ and $n(1-\hat{p}) \geq 5$. For small samples or extreme $p$, the Wilson interval provides superior coverage by solving the inequality $|(\hat{p}-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}| \leq z_{\alpha/2}$ directly for $p$.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import *

p_hat, n_val = Rational(58, 100), 400
z = Float(1.96)
se = sqrt(p_hat * (1 - p_hat) / n_val)
lower, upper = p_hat - z*se, p_hat + z*se
print(f"95% CI: ({float(lower):.4f}, {float(upper):.4f})")
